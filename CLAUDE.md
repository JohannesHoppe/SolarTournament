# SolarTournament – developer introduction

SolarTournament is a space shooter in the browser: three.js, TypeScript, Vite, hosted as a static site. Short rounds (5 minutes), ships fight each other in an asteroid field. Multiplayer runs peer-to-peer without a game server.

The original from 2012 (CopperLicht, Node.js, Socket.IO) lives on the branch `old-main-2013` (and its farewell page on `old-gh-pages-2013`). It only serves as a source of assets and ideas and is never changed.

**Language:** everything in this repository is English: code, comments, docs, commit messages. Johannes and Claude talk German with each other; that does not change the language of the repo.

**The game in four languages:** English, German, Spanish, French (see `src/i18n/i18n.ts`). Every text the player sees is written in English where it is used and wrapped in `t('…')` (placeholders in braces, `t('Gate {n} of {total}', { n, total })`; whole phrases, never fragments put together); a text kept in a table and shown elsewhere is marked with `msg('…')` and shown through `t(value)`; a fixed text of a page carries `data-i18n` (attributes `data-i18n-attr`). The flight computer's and the announcer's lines in `src/music/lines.ts` are translated where they are spoken. Each new or changed text gets its translation in `src/i18n/de.ts`, `es.ts` and `fr.ts` in the same commit (keyed by the exact English text; `npm run i18n -- de` lists what is missing); the unit tests fail on a missing, stale or broken entry. Translations follow the catalogs' glossaries and voices (the Host addressed as du/tú/tu; the President's bombast, the announcer's cynicism, Nicole's flirt); larger batches go to translators briefed on the universe.

**No foreign projects as references:** files and commit messages never name another project that served as a model or inspiration (for a license, a workflow, a pattern). Only what the repo actually uses is named: dependencies, assets and their licenses.

## Guideline: a universe of its own

SolarTournament has a universe of its own. We take inspiration from popular series, but only adopt two game principles:

- **A: Realistic physics as in The Expanse.** Reason: it simply was an awesome series. Whatever flies, collides, burns and breaks behaves the way it would out there, as long as it is fun: Newtonian flight, no sound in vacuum, no shields, lasers punch through. Where we deliberately deviate for the fun of it (e.g. the exaggerated gravity of the asteroids), the README says so explicitly.
- **B: Consciousness transfer into clones as in Altered Carbon, with one deliberate deviation.** Clones, printed by 3D printers, fly the ships; when a clone dies, the next one takes over. The ships are never printed: they are old fighters from the depots of centuries of war, used up in the Games, which is why none of them looks new (only the President's battleship is fresh from the dock). That explains the respawn. Mind the perspective in every text: the clone in the cockpit is the **pilot** (it flies, fights, feels the g-forces and dies); the one at the other end of the link who receives all of it, pays for the next clone and remembers every death is the **Host**. The player is addressed as the Host, never as "player". In the docs, "host" means only that: the announcer of Radio 2584 is the announcer, and the browser running an online round is the lead browser. Unlike Altered Carbon, where a stored memory is loaded into a new body (two bodies, two memories; the death itself is not remembered unless it is uploaded), the player, called the **Host**, is bound to the clones by a **live link**. The clone acts on its own, because radio links lag and only a mind in the cockpit reacts fast enough; but the link's bandwidth streams everything the clone lives through back into the Host's memory as it happens. When the clone dies, the Host holds all its memories and pain, the death included, and has to cope with it. The memory persists from clone to clone, dying hurts, and the soul rots a little more each time. This follows the reprinted expendable of the film Mickey 17.

On top of that, a look:

- **C: Neon and glitch, inspired by cyberpunk.** The future is screwed, and this is what it looks like: neon lettering, flickering tubes, sponsors everywhere, a picture that breaks up with every hit. Stylistic elements of well-known cyberpunk games may resonate, but without overdoing it; we have a style of our own, slightly retro (80s, old-school techno). Neon is a must.

We do not adopt names, terms or characters from the series and games; we fill the world with our own ideas.

### Music

Radio 2584 follows the maintainer's taste, written down in the README section "Radio 2584" ("Musical direction"): early techno, Kraftwerk, Daft Punk, hard beats, underground feeling, now and then German words with a rolling R. New musical decisions go there.

### Colors

Sun orange `#ff9d00` (hue 37°) for everything you press and everything that hits; its complement azure blue (217°) for information: `#0062ff` exactly as neon glow, `#4d91ff` lightened as readable text. Neon is a tinted white tube with layered `text-shadow`s in shades of the color (`--neon-orange`, `--neon-blue` in `style.css`); flickering respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. Use neon deliberately: the "Tournament" of the logo, plate headings, the sponsor sign, the radio title.

The HUD is the flight computer's projection and takes one color: azure (`--hud` and its shades in `style.css`), thin lines and letters with a soft glow and a dark hairline for readability, numbers in a fixed-width face. Orange in the HUD only for what the pilot has to act on now (a drive under the crosshair, a hit, a warning, the keys a drill asks for), red only for the ship in danger. What the flight computer draws into the world (the racing line, the flight school's markers, the reference plane's grid and the drop lines) is HUD too; the grid and the drop lines are an overlay drawn over everything, never hidden by rock, and kept faint; the world itself (gates, rocks, ships and their markers' tints) keeps its colors.

## Mandatory: every game principle is documented

Everything we come up with for the game must be documented, so that we can understand the SolarTournament universe thoroughly later: physics, damage model, rules, points, story, world.

- **README.md** explains the universe and the game physics for players and developers, with the actual numbers.
- Every change to a game rule or constant updates the README in the same commit. README and code must never drift apart.
- New story elements (time, world, characters, background) go into the README section "The universe".
- Constants in the code carry a comment with unit and reasoning.
- **The Pilot's Handbook** (`handbook.html`) tells the Hosts the rules they play by, with tactics, in all four languages. A change to a rule players use (controls, flight, weapons, items, a mode, an arena, points, the hangar) updates the handbook in the same commit, with its translations. Its numbers are placeholders filled from `src/handbook/numbers.ts`: from the game's constants where the page can import them, else from a number whose README wording the unit test finds; a digit written into a handbook text fails the test. Only what is built goes in; what the README says is coming stays out.

## Units

1 world unit = 5 cm. A ship is about 35 m long. Speeds in code in units/s, in the HUD in m/s (factor 0.05). 1 g = 196.2 units/s².

## Commands

```
npm install
npm run dev         # dev server on http://127.0.0.1:5173/
npm run typecheck
npm run dupes       # duplicated code (jscpd) and duplicated style rules (tools/dupes/css.ts)
npm test            # unit tests of the deterministic core (Vitest, in Node, under a second)
npm run e2e         # browser tests (Playwright: solo start, two tabs online); first once: npm run e2e:install
                    # DEV_PORT=5174 moves the dev server and the browser tests, so a second checkout (a worktree) tests beside the first
                    # the tests start their own dev server and stop if the port is taken; E2E_REUSE=1 tests against this checkout's server already running
npm run balance     # balance runs (Playwright, about forty minutes): every mode played out by bots in time-lapse in every arena
npm run soak        # the cheat checks in honest play (Playwright, about a quarter of an hour): three tabs, a round of every mode, nobody reported
npm run bench       # benchmark (Playwright, about ten minutes): the cost per frame of every feature, quality rung, arena and mode
                    # PROFILE=pixel8 as the reference phone (its screen, touch, its CPU speed); BENCH_SECONDS=<s> per measurement; BENCH_ONLY=quality for one section
npm run build       # static site in dist/ (renders the voice, checks types, duplicates and unit tests first)
npm run check       # every quick quality gate in one run, summed up: types, duplicates, unit tests (tools/check/)
npm run check:full  # the same, then the browser tests and the balance runs
npm run relays      # checks public Nostr relays and rewrites src/net/relays.json when the healthy ones changed (tools/relays/, weekly by a workflow)
```

## How to work

The same loop for every change, small or large. Follow it step by step; it holds everything that is easy to forget.

1. **Start.** `git pull`, then `npm run dev`. The dev server renders the voices first: quick from the cache, but many minutes after spoken lines changed. When only code changed and the voices are rendered, `node_modules/.bin/vite` starts the server at once.
2. **Fast loop while coding.**
   - `npm run typecheck` (a second) and `npm test` (a few seconds) after every step.
   - The dev server reloads the page by itself.
   - In the browser, `window.game` reads and sets the game's state (see Testing in the browser).
   - `game.log.summary()` sums up every death by its cause and the bots' decisions before it.
3. **Diagnose, never guess.** A balance run or a bug is explained from the dev log, a measurement or a test, never from a plausible story. What is not measured is called unknown.
   - **A change to how something is drawn is looked at.** A shader, a baked texture, a level of detail or a quality rung is checked on screenshots of what it changes, at the rungs it touches and drawn every way the game draws it (a mesh of its own, instanced, a billboard), before and after; a benchmark measures what a picture costs, never whether it is right. What it bakes gets a test that reads it back the way the GPU does.
4. **Texts.**
   - Every text the player sees or hears is written in English where it is used and wrapped in `t()`, `msg()` or `data-i18n`.
   - It gets its German, Spanish and French in the same commit; `npm run i18n -- de` (`es`, `fr`) lists what is missing, and the unit tests fail on a gap.
   - Spoken lines are cloned in Nicole's voice by the workflow Voice after the push; until then they fall back to Kokoro.
5. **Docs in the same commit.**
   - README: every rule with its numbers.
   - `handbook.html`: every rule players use, its tactics and numbers (see the Pilot's Handbook above), translated.
   - The folder's README: every file and what it does.
   - The structure table below.
   - `datenschutz.html`: every new data flow or storage key.
6. **Before every commit.**
   - `npm run check`: types, duplicates, unit tests, summed up in a table.
   - A change to the flight model, the flight computer, the input or the HUD's steering cues (the course marker, the crosshair, the racing line) has to pass the simulated Host's tests (`src/game/playability.test.ts`, `hostrace.test.ts`, `hostrace2.test.ts`, part of `npm test`): a human with ordinary hands must be able to fly the race and the flight school. Never loosen them to make a change pass; fix the change.
   - Commit with explicit paths (`git add <files>`, never `-A`), with an English message that says what the change does and why; no attribution lines.
7. **The full gates.**
   - `npm run check:full` runs everything above, plus the browser tests and the balance runs (about an hour).
   - Run it after every change to bots, weapons, damage, a mode's rules, the network or the round flow, and in any case every few merges. The game changes fast, and only the full run catches a rule that quietly breaks a mode, a race nobody finishes or two tabs that stop agreeing.
   - The balance runs leave the dev log of every round in `test-results/`, and a failure names the deaths by cause. `BALANCE_SLOW` gives them more time on a busy machine, `BALANCE_PAGES` plays fewer rounds side by side (see `e2e/README.md`).
   - A red gate is fixed before the next push, never ignored.
8. **Push.** `git push origin main main:stage_prod` (see Hosting below for when `stage_prod` stays behind).
9. **Parallel work in worktrees.**
   - Every checkout runs its own dev server on its own port (`DEV_PORT=5174 npm run e2e`).
   - jscpd skips `.claude/worktrees` (ignored by git), so a worktree checks its duplicates on a copy outside it.
   - A worktree merges `main` before it reports.
   - Doc conflicts are resolved keeping both sides, one table row per line and one paragraph per bullet.
   - Benchmarks run one at a time on a quiet machine (1-minute load average below 3, no other browser busy), never beside another benchmark, test run or the code they are compared with; their numbers mean nothing otherwise. Every browser, tab and dev server a check opens is closed once it is done.
   - Many browsers at once on one machine slow each other down. The GPU-less test browser renders the title screen's pad and plays the audio in every tab, so a test that times out under load is rerun before it is called broken.

**Tests.** The unit tests (`src/**/*.test.ts`, helpers in `src/test/`) cover what every browser must compute alike and what guards the game: songs (same title, same notes, a jump lands on the same step, every note on the scale), the arena of a round, the field (same layout, snapshot and restore, orbits, a rock colliding where it is seen, box and well collision), the race course and its gates (its bends gentle enough to be flown at the speed limit), the autopilot with realistic input (a scripted hand on the mouse turning the nose, a turn about, free drift to autopilot, switching modes, race bends at full speed, a recorded flight replayed; the flight back within 15° of the nose within a second after a hard turn at every speed, the nose never leading it farther than its limit, free drift keeping the flight), the modes of a room, the clones and the flare of Last Clone Standing, the sides, bases and cores of Capture the Flag, the President's battleship (its parts, phases, plume, the cover of its hull, torpedo hits and sinking), **playability** (a simulated Host with mouse and keyboard, `src/test/human.ts`, through the game's input, flight computer and chase camera, a quarter second late and a few pixels off: a lap of every arena's race with hands from quick to slow, never lost, the flight school's drills, a turn about, a crossing ship held under the crosshair), the lap timer and its ghost, the hangar's wallet and what of a kept hangar only an edit puts there (never an unreadable entry, never anything the game itself keeps), the hook's cable, probes, wreckage, the ships' tints (a bright palette clear of the HUD's and the sides' colors, one tint per bot, the tie rule between Hosts), comets (the same comets and showers on every browser, rock broken and hull shoved at the same field times however the frames fall, a newcomer's snapshot carrying the shoved hull, the black hole and the gas giant bending and swallowing them, the battleship getting out of their way), the Satellite Junkyard's sky (night beneath it on Earth, the arena itself in sunlight, its junk beyond any ship's reach), the checks of messages from other browsers, room links, the shared link and text by where the Host is (menu, school, solo, a public and a private tournament) and the networks' share links, Nicole's coaching (each tip only so often), the Pilot's Handbook's numbers (taken from the game's constants or found in the README, none written into its texts). **Plausibility:** what a Host is asked to fly, a bot of middling skill (0.5) must manage; `src/game/courses.test.ts` flies it with the game's flight model, gravity, coils and rock collisions (`src/test/pilot.ts`): a lap of every arena's race course along its ideal line without being lost, and every course of the flight school (the curve, the ring, coil, brake and full-speed drills) from the arena's middle and from its edge, inside the arena, missing no ring (every coil through its core, the full-speed course within its time), the brake drill's bend tighter than the autopilot turns at the speed limit and the full-speed course's gentler, and the slide shot drill's bot long enough off the flight to hit. A new course, drill or arena gets such a test. The modes get theirs in the balance runs (`e2e/balance.spec.ts`, `npm run balance`, not part of `npm run e2e`): every mode of a solo round in every arena (a deathmatch 24 times each, Last Clone Standing six times, Capture the Flag twice, the race and the battleship once), seven rounds side by side, played out by the default rivals in time-lapse with the Host's ship taken out, so that chance does not decide them; the middling bot (Kessler), judged on its mean over all rounds of a mode, scores at least the median (in a race, where every bot flies the line alike, it finishes; in Last Clone Standing it places fifth or better) and dies on average at most four times in a five-minute deathmatch, five times in Capture the Flag and three times in a race and in the President's Fleet, a deathmatch sees six kills, Capture the Flag a capture, and the bots sink the battleship. Run them after every change to bots, weapons, damage or a mode's rules. The cheat checks must never take an honest Host for a cheater: `src/net/plausible.test.ts` flies honest ships wildly with the game's flight model through blows, stutters and stalled links (`src/test/honest.ts`), and the soak (`e2e/soak.spec.ts`, `npm run soak`) plays a round of every mode in three honest tabs; run it after every change to the checks, the flight model, or what can push, spin or move a ship. Every change to these rules adds or updates a test; `npm run build` fails on a red one. The browser tests (`e2e/`, against the dev server) start a solo round of every mode and each course of the flight school without errors, buy an upgrade in the hangar and keep it, turn the flight about at 150 m/s with the mouse, fly the first gates of a race with the mouse, name a hangar edited to what no Host can own until it is put back, and let two tabs of one tournament see the same arena, round, rocks and ships (in the same tints), a torpedo of one hit the other, a probe on one's hook come in to its ship and be taken there, agree on the sides and cores of Capture the Flag, and see one battleship and count the same hits on it, a laser's and a torpedo's, draw a rock alike as an instance and as a mesh, and open the Pilot's Handbook in every language, and find the share button on every screen and page, its menu copying the game's link or, in an online room, the room's. Run them before pushing changes to the network or the round flow. How the game feels and sounds is tested by playing.

Hosting is a classic Cloudflare Pages project (`wrangler.toml`), never Workers, built by Cloudflare from every push (Git integration). Its one piece of server code is a Pages Function of the same project, `functions/turn.ts` (`GET /turn`): it hands out short-lived TURN credentials and keeps the TURN key on the server; the key comes from the Pages project's environment variables `TURN_KEY_ID` and `TURN_KEY_API_TOKEN` (secret), set by Johannes in the Cloudflare dashboard for production and preview. Cloudflare has no spending cap for TURN, so the function keeps its own monthly budget (`MONTHLY_BUDGET_GB`): it reads the month's relay traffic through the GraphQL analytics with `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` (a plain variable in `wrangler.toml`: with that file, Pages takes plain variables only from it, secrets still from the dashboard) and `TURN_ANALYTICS_TOKEN` (secret, Account Analytics Read) and hands out nothing when the budget is spent or the traffic cannot be read. Branches: `main` is the test stage (https://test.solartournament.games), `stage_prod` is production (https://solartournament.games). Every page sends visitors of the raw Pages addresses (`solartournament.pages.dev`, `main.solartournament.pages.dev`) on to these domains with a small inline script, keeping path and room code; the plugin `pageShell` in `vite.config.ts` writes it into every page (also into the static `public/404.html` in `dist/`), together with what else the pages share (icon, stylesheet, the seal behind the station pages, the head of the legal pages), and the build writes `dist/_headers` with the Content Security Policy (the inline script allowed by its hash; a new inline script, an external source or a new kind of connection needs the policy in `vite.config.ts` changed too). Work happens on `main`. As long as the game has no real players yet, every push goes to both branches at once (`git push origin main main:stage_prod`); once it has players, `stage_prod` only moves when Johannes asks for it.

Never use `npx`, always the scripts from `package.json`.

## Structure

The repository root is the Vite project: `index.html` (the game), `radio.html` (Radio 2584), `handbook.html` (the Pilot's Handbook, linked from the menu, the flight school's plate and the pause menu, which opens it at the section of the mode flown), `impressum.html` and `datenschutz.html` (German legal pages, linked from the menu and the radio page) are its pages, `public/` holds the static files, `tools/` the generators that write into `public/`.

**The privacy policy follows the code.** `datenschutz.html` lists every data flow of the game: Cloudflare hosting, the thirteen localStorage entries, the online tournament and its lobby (WebRTC between players, public Nostr relays, Cloudflare's STUN server and TURN relay, public tournament names, the cheat reports between the browsers), the share menu (nothing sent until the Host picks a target; the networks it links to), the satire ads (no ad network), e-mail contact. Any change that adds a data flow (a new storage key, an external request, an ad network, analytics, another TURN or STUN server) updates the privacy policy in the same commit. Nothing is loaded from third-party servers at runtime.

**Every folder has a README** that says what the folder is for, what each file in it does and how it is used or run, short and for a human finding their way around. A new folder gets one, and a file added, removed or changed in what it does updates its folder's README in the same commit. The build leaves the READMEs in `public/` out of the site.

Sprites (keyed by a hash of their inputs), the clones, ffmpeg and the Kokoro model (one folder per revision, used only once a download has completed) are kept in `_solartournament/` inside npm's cache directory (`~/.npm` by default): Cloudflare Pages keeps that directory between builds and no other, so a fresh checkout whose inputs did not change copies the sprite instead of rendering it (`VOICE_CACHE` moves the cache; where it cannot be written, the voice is rendered all the same). Never commit the sprite: generated binaries bloat the repository's history for good.

## Code rules

- **Determinism:** everything that concerns the shared world state (the arena of a round, its field, fragments, wreckage, the notes of Radio 2584) comes from seeded randomness (`Randomizer`), never from `Math.random()`. Otherwise peer-to-peer players see different worlds. `Math.random()` is only allowed for visuals (particles, flicker, glitches) and bot decisions.
- **Human and alien ship are identical**, only their looks differ (shared hit radius); so is the Sovereign. Upgrades bought in the hangar change a ship for its own Host only, both interceptors alike, and online only up to their cap (see `game/hangar.ts`).
- **Bots obey the Hosts' rules.** A bot moves its ship only through a Host's controls (stick, drive, thrusters, trigger, and the actions a Host has, with their cooldowns), its clone takes the same strain, its clones wait as long, and it knows only what a Host's sensors show (line of sight, sensor ranges, storms, the lock warning). Only its decisions are its own (AI and skills). A new rule or ability applies to bots the same way; `src/ship/bots.test.ts` guards it.
- **No sound from outside:** the player hears only what travels through the own hull, plus the flight computer's beeps, the radio and the link static in the headset.
- **Performance:** no objects created per frame in hot loops; reuse module-level scratch vectors.
- **Write nothing twice.** Code, markup and styles exist once: a repeated stretch becomes a function, a constant, a selector list or part of the page shell (`vite.config.ts`). `npm run dupes` checks it: jscpd (`.jscpd.json`: `src`, `tools`, `e2e`, `functions`, the four pages, `public/404.html` and `vite.config.ts`, from 5 lines and 50 tokens, threshold 0, files of any length) and `tools/dupes/css.ts`, which finds style rules with the same set of at least three declarations under different selectors. Run it regularly and at the latest before every commit; `npm run build` fails on a duplicate, so Cloudflare does not deploy one. A repetition that is technically unavoidable is marked with `jscpd:ignore-start` / `jscpd:ignore-end` (in a style sheet the comment `css-dupes: <reason>` before the rule) and one sentence why.

## Testing in the browser

In dev mode the game hangs on the window as `window.game`. It lets you read and set state (Playwright, `browser_evaluate`):

- Start a solo round: `document.getElementById('start').click()` opens its choices, `document.getElementById('solo-launch').click()` launches it
- Start a course of the flight school: `document.getElementById('school-racer').click()` (also `school-basic`, `school-gladiator`, `school-arsenal`, `school-tactician`); to start one at a drill, set `localStorage['solartournament.school-step']` to `{"arsenal": "probe"}` (the course and the drill's name, its key in `DRILL` in `game/school.ts`; "opening" and "closing" for a course's first and last words) before
- Simulate keys: `window.dispatchEvent(new KeyboardEvent('keydown', { code: 'KeyW' }))`
- Freeze time for screenshots: `game.timeScale = 0`
- Disable bots: set the other ships to `alive = false` and `respawnAt = Infinity`
- Multiplayer: open two tabs with `?local=1#t=<code>` (at least six letters or digits) and click "Join tournament" in both (BroadcastChannel, no internet); without `local=1` the same test runs over Trystero and real Nostr relays. Several tabs render too slowly without a GPU (frame gaps make browsers start over, see `CUT_OFF_MS`): switch rendering off with `game.composer.render = () => {}`. Cut a tab's link with `const t = game.online.session.transport; t._post = t.post; t.post = () => {}; t._recv = t.channel.onmessage; t.channel.onmessage = null` (restore both to reconnect); freeze it with a busy loop of a few seconds
- Render music offline: `const { renderPreview } = await import('/src/music/preview.ts')`, then `renderPreview('Orbit Terror', 'round', undefined, fromSecond, seconds)` (the title is the seed; a flavor can be forced); returns a WAV file as base64 that can be saved and listened to
- Read the development log (`src/devlog.ts`, only in development, cleared at every round's start): `game.log.summary()` counts the round's deaths by cause (rock, hull, gate, wreck, ram, knockout, laser, torpedo, nova, plume, flare, comet, shower, well, space, …), by ship, by what the victim was doing and by the decisions of victim and killer in the 3 seconds before, contacts between ships by outcome (bump, shove, ram) and shoves, the torpedoes launched and where they went off (on a ship, on a part, on the hull, on rock, burnt out; with a lost lock or a dead shooter), and in the President's Fleet the hits on the battleship by part and weapon, when each part broke and when it went critical; `game.log.filter('death')` (or `'decision'`, `'contact'`, `'shove'`, `'act'`, `'fleet'`, or a function) lists the events, each death with position, speed, impact speed, killer and those decisions; `game.log.json()` is all of it. Every bot logs each change of its decision (race, attack, defend, flee, siege, probe, roam; avoiding rock, giving way to a ship) with its reason, and its acts (shove, hook, torpedo); every torpedo where it went off (`torpedo gone`). The balance runs attach each round's log to the result (`test-results/`) and put the deaths by cause into a failure's detail
- Record a flight (`src/recorder.ts`, only in development, cleared at every round's start): the last minute of the own ship, step by step, with the Host's controls, the mouse pointer, the commands the flight computer gave and the ship before each step. `game.recorder.download()` saves it as JSON (`download(10)` the last 10 seconds), `game.recorder.recording()` returns it; `replay()` in `src/test/cockpit.ts` flies it again through the autopilot in a unit test
- Trigger a glitch: `game.glitch.trigger(1, 5)`; idle glitches in the menu make element screenshots fail, so stop them with `game.nextIdleGlitch = Infinity`
- The picture's quality (`src/view/quality.ts`, README "Performance"): `game.graphics.rung` and `game.graphics.look` say what is drawn, `game.graphics.choose('low')` pins a tier (`'auto'` hands it back to the governor); the browser tests pin `low` in `localStorage['solartournament.quality']` before the page loads (`e2e/game.ts`). `?fps` shows the frame rate and the rung at the bottom of the screen; `?bench` (or `?bench=<seconds>`) runs the benchmark in the page, `npm run bench` in the test browser (`PROFILE=pixel8` as the reference phone)

The test browser renders without a GPU and composites the page without it (`--disable-gpu-compositing`, see `playwright.config.ts`); its frame rate says nothing about real hardware, the benchmark's table in it only compares costs with each other. Game feel and sound cannot be judged this way; Johannes tests those himself.
