# src/test

Helpers for the unit tests (`npm test`, Vitest in Node), not part of the game.

- `cube.ts`: reads a baked cube map back the way the GPU does (the face and texel a direction picks, the direction a texel stands for, a texel's normal) and a detail tile's seams and means, and checks that a shape's map is its own, square and mipmapped, and baked alike every time; the tests of the relief maps of rock, ice and the comet's nucleus use it.
- `field.ts`: `driftProbes` moves the probes a step bouncing off a field and its battleship, as the game does; an asteroid field with the real rock shapes (in the detail a test asks for), rock and ice breaking into pieces of themselves cut at that detail, and plain boxes for hull pieces, or the world ship's own collider boxes (`HULL_BOXES`); in the Belt's layout its rigs and buoys stand solid on their rocks, as the game dresses them (see world/mining, `standClaims`).
- `honest.ts`: an honest ship flown wildly with the game's flight model (slammed sticks, flips, the autopilot, beams), blown about by shoves, hits and venting air, on a browser that stutters and a link that stalls; returns its states and beams as another browser receives them, for the cheat checks (`src/net/plausible.test.ts`) to prove they never take it for a cheat.
- `cockpit.ts`: the own ship as the game flies it, without the game (flight model and the autopilot, free drift or the brake); a Host's hand on the mouse that turns the nose onto a goal in eased strokes, a moment late, overshooting and correcting; how a flight came onto a direction (time, overshoot, nose), the slip between flight and nose; and the replay of a recording of the input recorder (`src/recorder.ts`) through the autopilot. The scenarios of `src/ship/flight.test.ts` use it.
- `human.ts`: a simulated Host at mouse and keyboard: it sees the screen through the game's chase camera, steers the crosshair (and the prograde marker) onto what it wants to fly to a quarter second late, in eased strokes, a few pixels off, and flies through the game's input mapping and flight computer; `racer` races a course by the ribbon with drive and brake as a Host would; `raceLaps` flies an arena's lap with hands from quick to slow. The playability tests use it.
- `racehost.ts`: the race test of an arena for the simulated Host, shared by `src/game/hostrace.test.ts` and `hostrace2.test.ts`, which run side by side.
- `pilot.ts`: flies a course of rings with the game's flight model, gravity, coils and rock collisions, and in a planet's air its drag and the heat it puts into the hull (a hull it burns up is lost), like a racing bot of a given skill (along the line, and for a ring's center near it, as the game's racers do), from rest or drifting, and counts the rings passed and missed (`flyCourse` flies any driver, a bot or the simulated Host; a test may look at the flight after every step and end it, as the cover drill's test does while a torpedo homes on the bot); the plausibility tests (`src/game/courses.test.ts`) use it to prove a course can be flown.
