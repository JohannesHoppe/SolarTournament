# src/test

Helpers for the unit tests (`npm test`, Vitest in Node), not part of the game.

- `bigfloat.ts`: binary floating point of any precision in integers (`BigInt`): π by Machin's formula, ln 2 by its series, and every function of `src/detmath.ts` from its defining series to 192 bits, the reference `src/detmath.test.ts` measures the errors in ulps against.
- `cube.ts`: reads a baked cube map back the way the GPU does (the face and texel a direction picks, the direction a texel stands for, a texel's normal) and a detail tile's seams and means, and checks that a shape's map is its own, square and mipmapped, and baked alike every time; the tests of the relief maps of rock, ice and the comet's nucleus use it.
- `field.ts`: `driftProbes` moves the probes a step bouncing off a field and its battleship, as the game does; an asteroid field with the real rock shapes (in the detail a test asks for), rock and ice breaking into pieces of themselves cut at that detail, and plain boxes for hull pieces, or the world ship's own collider boxes (`HULL_BOXES`); in the Belt's and Rheasilvia's layouts their rigs and buoys stand solid on their rocks, as the game dresses them (see world/mining, `standClaims`), and `claimsIn` reads their claims as the game's mining does; a field of nothing but a moon's ground and where it fails to collide as its surface gives it (`groundField`), and where its relief leaves its box (`reliefOutside`).
- `jobs.ts`: a headless stand-in for the game around the flight school, `JobWorld`, the drills' `SchoolContext`: a job of the campaign flown in Node through its drills' own code and the real `FlightSchool`, in the place's real field (gravity, air, ground, the pieces meeting each other), the own clone as a `Ship` in the campaign's hull with the game's flight computer (the brake, the autopilot, the aim assist), every other ship of a drill a `Bot` set up as the game sets it up (drones, sparring bots, raiders, rivals, wingmen, the flock), lasers along the nose through rock, hulls and the drill's props, torpedoes, the own hook on rock and ships, the orbit readout, the bots going easy on a Host who keeps dying; a line takes its time to say, by its words. Left out: wreckage, probes, the bots' hooks and decoys, scenes in person (the clone waits in its clamps), the comets' showers outside the drills' own. `flyJob` flies a job and tells how each drill went (seconds, attempts, the lines that started one over, attempts that failed at once) and how the clone died.
- `jobhost.ts`: the simulated Host of the jobs, `host`: a pilot of middling skill whose hands go through the flight model, reading what the HUD shows: it holds still while a line is said, flies to a drill's marker and holds there (in free drift, round what is in the way, stopping short of a surface, keeping up with a marker that moves, slow in a planet's air, escorting a hull from above it), races a course of rings or pipes as the game's rivals do, and fights what is hostile as a bot of its skill does, with the Host's trigger and the shooting down of a torpedo homing on it.
- `jobbehaviors.ts`: what the simulated Host does in a drill that asks for a maneuver of its own (cutting rock for its ore, shooting the Repo Men's winch, the gel at the launch ring, scanning Scout One, the hook's swing, riding the comet, towing Scout One off its route, an orbit about the hole, flying a groove low, climbing the bridge tower's hatches), by job and drill.
- `jobsuite.ts`: the tests of the campaign's jobs (`npm run jobs`, in the files `src/campaign/jobs.*.test.ts` side by side): every job flown by the simulated Host with the campaign as a Host has it then, every drill done within its attempts and the clone lost to the place itself no more than a few times; never a clone printed in rock, never an attempt failing before a Host can act; the jobs a middling bot does not win as built (`OUTMATCHED`, mostly fights of one against several) with what was measured. `JOB=<id>` flies one, `TRACE=<s>` traces it, `STRICT=1` holds the outmatched to winning too.
- `honest.ts`: an honest ship flown wildly with the game's flight model (slammed sticks, flips, the autopilot, beams), blown about by shoves, hits and venting air, on a browser that stutters and a link that stalls; returns its states and beams as another browser receives them, for the cheat checks (`src/net/plausible.test.ts`) to prove they never take it for a cheat.
- `cockpit.ts`: the own ship as the game flies it, without the game (flight model and the autopilot, free drift or the brake); a Host's hand on the mouse that turns the nose onto a goal in eased strokes, a moment late, overshooting and correcting; how a flight came onto a direction (time, overshoot, nose), the slip between flight and nose; and the replay of a recording of the input recorder (`src/recorder.ts`) through the autopilot. The scenarios of `src/ship/flight.test.ts` use it.
- `human.ts`: a simulated Host at mouse and keyboard: it sees the screen through the game's chase camera, steers the crosshair (and the prograde marker) onto what it wants to fly to a quarter second late, in eased strokes, a few pixels off, and flies through the game's input mapping and flight computer; `racer` races a course by the ribbon with drive and brake as a Host would; `raceLaps` flies an arena's lap with hands from quick to slow. The playability tests use it.
- `racehost.ts`: the race test of an arena for the simulated Host, shared by `src/game/hostrace.test.ts` and `hostrace2.test.ts`, which run side by side.
- `pilot.ts`: flies a course of rings with the game's flight model, gravity, coils and rock collisions, and in a planet's air its drag and the heat it puts into the hull (a hull it burns up is lost), like a racing bot of a given skill (along the line, and for a ring's center near it, as the game's racers do), from rest or drifting, and counts the rings passed and missed (`flyCourse` flies any driver, a bot or the simulated Host; a test may look at the flight after every step and end it, as the cover drill's test does while a torpedo homes on the bot; with a cap the bot holds its speed under it, the drive off and the thrusters braking, as the Verona Drop asks); the plausibility tests (`src/game/courses.test.ts`) use it to prove a course can be flown.
