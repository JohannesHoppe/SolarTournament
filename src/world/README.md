# src/world

The arena a round is fought in. Everything that every browser must build alike (the field's layout, the rock shapes, fragments) comes from seeded randomness, so peers see the same world.

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `arenas.ts` | The seven arenas: field layout, well, air, sun, storms, the fixed references the HUD marks; which one a tournament round uses; the default arena (the Rings of Saturn) |
| `air.ts` | Jupiter's air: its density by the altitude over the cloud deck, the haze summed along a line and how far the eye, the laser, the radar and a lock reach through it (the deck a veil on top), the drag on a ship over a step, the heat in its hull and the safe speed by depth |
| `reference.ts` | The reference plane through the field's center: height, climb, the bank's sky pointer, closure rate, an arena's fixed references |
| `asteroids.ts` | The field: rocks, ice, debris or hull (shoved by a comet), a disk with pieces in the open space above and below it, orbits, gravity, collisions, raycasts, line of sight; how close up a rock or an ice piece keeps its near mesh before the far one, then a billboard, takes over (the picture's quality) |
| `rocks.ts` | Generates the 16 asteroid shapes with integer math, identical on every browser: a full-detail shape for collisions (its vertices only, never drawn), light near and far meshes for drawing (their fine relief a baked, object-space normal map, not triangles), and a billboard (see view/impostor) for the belt and for rocks and ice far from the field |
| `surface.ts` | A rock's measured surface, as collisions see it |
| `bodies.ts` | Bodies and their masses (ship, core, probe, torpedo, wreckage, the field's pieces by volume and density, the battleship; rings immovable); the rope that only pulls (between two browsers as a stiff spring, each end pulled where it is run) and the bump, both split by mass |
| `shapes.ts` | Ice, the Orbital Freedom Network's junk (satellites, upper stages, fairing halves, stacks) and the world ship's hull sections |
| `gates.ts` | The rings of the tournament as structures (coil housings, windings, struts, lights, a station module with a sponsor's plate), all solid but the lights, shared by race gates, school rings and Capture the Flag bases |
| `powercore.ts` | The sponsor core of Capture the Flag as a structure: an armored reactor canister with glowing windings, sign plates and a cage for the tow cable; fades to its outline |
| `boxes.ts` | A sphere or a ray against a box (wreck and battleship hulls) |
| `wrecks.ts` | The pieces a destroyed ship breaks into |
| `arena.ts` | The arena's edge and its grid |
| `belt.ts` | The asteroid belt beyond the arena, scenery only; close to its inner edge real low-poly rocks, the rest of the ring billboards (see view/impostor); a cheaper picture draws a share of it |
| `mining.ts` | The Rockborn's mining in the Belt: rigs with work lights, cutting lasers with sparks and a glowing cut, dust from drill holes, rocks cut half away with smooth faces and lit tunnels, beacon buoys, neon graffiti, and dust clouds between the rocks glowing against the sun; placed from the field's seed on the rocks' measured surfaces, turning with its rocks through a uniform array of their matrices, seven draw calls in all, thinner by the picture's quality; how thick the drill dust is where the own ship flies, for the patter on its hull |
| `jupiter.ts` | Jupiter besides its map: the cloud deck as a see-through sheet seen from both sides, two thinner sheets under it and the haze's floor below them, lightning, the auroras over the south pole, Io and Europa; the haze's light from the map's colors around the arena; fewer sheets on a cheaper picture |
| `landmarks.ts` | Each arena's scenery: planets, the sun, the black hole. Saturn and Jupiter from real maps (`public/assets/planets/`), sharp or light by the picture's quality (see view/quality), Jupiter's cap (its cloud deck, made by `jupiter.ts`) at a cheaper segment count on a cheaper picture; Saturn's ring with the planet's shadow on it and its own on the planet, worked out in the shaders; the ring's sheet, the specks of ice around the pilot that show its layer, and the grains of the layer close by (streaking past a ship flying through, fewer on a cheaper picture), lit by the sun's shadow map, so everything in the field shadows them, and glinting |
| `junkyard.ts` | The Satellite Junkyard's scenery: Earth at its real size below at night from its night map (`public/assets/planets/earth-*.jpg`), its lights grown into the 26th century, the air along its horizon, satellites burning up in it, drawn behind everything; the junk beyond the field on its orbits about Earth as one draw call of point sprites; the network's flickering billboard |
| `flare.ts` | The solar flare's plasma on screen: a sphere of roiling fire with filaments |
| `space.ts` | Stars, nebula, the sun's light and shadows (heated by a solar flare, dimmed by a planet's haze over the camera; their map's size or none at all, as the picture's quality says), the sky's light (taking the haze's colors under a cloud deck), dust and its streaks at speed (`dustStreaks`, also the dust of a comet's tail) |

`junkyard.test.ts` checks that the junkyard lies in sunlight above Earth's night and that its junk stays beyond the reach of any ship; `mining.test.ts` that the Belt's mining stands alike on every browser, on whole rocks only, its cut faces never more than their tolerance above the measured surface, its drill holes in the surface and its dust clouds clear of the rocks; `asteroids.test.ts`, `arenas.test.ts` and `wrecks.test.ts` check layouts, snapshots and collisions (and that no field piece switches to its billboard within the arena across), `bodies.test.ts` the rope and the bump, `air.test.ts` Jupiter's air (density, the eye's reach by depth, the haze along a line against a ray's reach, the deck hiding a ship under it, drag, the speed the drive holds against it, heat and the safe speed, the hull burnt only by what full radiators cannot shed), `reference.test.ts` the reference plane in every arena, height, climb, the sky pointer, the closure rate and each arena's fixed references, `rocks.test.ts` that a rock's normal atlas, read row by row as the GPU reads it, holds the outward normal of every cell's direction and that the shapes the collisions measure are the drawn rocks vertex for vertex (`npm test`).
