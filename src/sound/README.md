# src/sound

What the pilot hears besides the radio. There is no sound in vacuum: everything comes muffled through the hull, except the flight computer's beeps and voice in the headset. Only in a planet's air (Jupiter's, under its cloud deck) does sound from outside come in, through the hull as well: the wind and thunder.

- `audio.ts`: the audio context as a media player's (`playbackContext`: iOS's silent mode leaves it alone, the radio page uses it too), the hull's low-pass, the drive's rumble, beeps, the link's static, dust pattering on the hull in a comet's tail (and ice grains in Saturn's ring, ice crystals at the Derelict's break, called from game/game.ts); in a planet's air the wind roaring by the air's push and the speed, the plasma roaring around a hot hull (and its plates crackling as they burn), and thunder after lightning (a crack close by, a long roll far off); low over a world's ground the hover field's rush through the hull (deep slow, a hiss fast; in air also a close pass by anything) and the grind of a hull gliding on the ground (`setGround`, from game/game.ts).
- `sounds.ts`: synthesized effects (laser, explosion, a gate's boost, the clonk of a decoy let go).
- `chatter.ts`: Nicole's chatter at the moments that come again and again in real rounds (a round's start, a clone lost, the next clone, a round's end, a race's places and passes, a capture): a pool of lines for each, never one of the last few of it again, a share of silence for each (more right after she spoke), a least gap between any two lines and a long one between two praises; warnings never pass through it. `chatter.test.ts` holds it to that.
- `speaker.ts`: Nicole, the flight computer: plays her lines from the pre-rendered voice sprite (thin, through a narrow band, in the campaign until it gives her own voice back: `thin`) (and the campaign's other people, Rask, Pim, Hedda, the Saw Brothers, Ma Tally, the Navy officer, Hollow Jack, Bellamy Crisp, Spare, Commissioner Quill, Admiral Vey, Perihelion, Kesh and the President, over a comm of their own: a narrow, gritty radio band), with lessons that are never cut off and chatter that gives way, and tells whether one of a set of lines is being said.

The voice itself is rendered ahead of time by `tools/voice/`.
