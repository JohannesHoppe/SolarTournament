# src/ship

The ships and who flies them. Human and alien interceptor (and the Sovereign and the Magpie) fly by the same rules; only their looks differ.

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `ship.ts` | A ship: its body (an interceptor's mass, see world/bodies), hull, hit zones, holes and decompression, laser heat (and how much its radiators shed where it is, and how they glow with it; another browser's ship warmed by the shots seen), the clone's g-force strain, drive animation, the puffs of its thrusters' nozzles (another browser's ship read from how its velocity and spin change); how one ship's nose lies on another, as the lock warning and the bots read it; the laser's reach and the first hull on a line, where a beam ends whichever side the ship is on; its state over the network, and how a new lead adopts a bot; silent running (`silent`, `detectable`: drive cold and no shot for 2 s, the sensors find it only within 1 km); a beam into a bare left flank (`Perks.bareFlank`, the campaign's hull before its armor panels) costs twice the armor's; its overdrive's recharge (`overdriveReadyAt`), a race's drive heat (`driveHeat`) and a burnout's harm from inside (`burnOut`); its way through a track's branches (`ways`) |
| `ship.test.ts` | A bot adopted by a new lead flies on with the torpedoes its last state told of, up to a fresh load; the count goes out with the state whenever it changes; a fresh clone's radiators start cold |
| `models.test.ts` | The mirrored half of a part keeps each vertex's color (the heat tint, the radiators' warmth) with its vertex |
| `flight.ts` | The Newtonian flight model and its flight computer (`steer()`), and the autopilot (`flyByNose`): the ship flies where its nose points, drive and thrusters taking out the drift across the nose, the nose leading the flight no farther than they bring it back within a second (`leadLimit`); the lift of fins and hull in a planet's air and the drag it costs (`aero`, `LIFT`, `LIFT_MOST`, `INDUCED`; the ship's air density in `airDensity`), counted by the autopilot as a pull from outside; the grip, the hardest turn at a speed, with the autopilot and in free drift, in air with its lift (`gripAt`, `turnRadius`, `cornerSpeed`); the autopilot riding a world's ground at the hover field's ride height (`HOVER_HEIGHT`, `HOVER_RIDE`); the overdrive's shove onto the crosshair's line, on a ram course at whatever lies there (`overdriveShove`) |
| `flight.test.ts` | The autopilot with realistic input (`src/test/cockpit.ts`): the nose at rest, the flight back on the nose after a turn at every speed and within the lead's limit, a hand turning the nose to a goal, a turn about at 150 m/s with the brake, the autopilot taking over from free drift, switching modes in a turn, gravity, the hardest turn and race bends at full speed; free drift keeping the flight; a recorded flight replaying the same |
| `computer.ts` | The own ship's flight computer besides the autopilot: the upright hold, the brake to rest, the aim assist; the game and the simulated Host of the tests fly with it |
| `impact.ts` | One rule for a hull meeting a body (`feltBlow`, `blowDamage`): the closing speed times the other body's share of the two masses, where it lands, through the rock's curve. Striking a surface: the bounce and the scrape (a bump of bodies, see world/bodies; a livelier body passes its own restitution), the damage; a ship meeting a piece of junk (`strikeJunk`): through a sheet, the plug it tears out taking its speed and denting the armor by its kinetic energy (`sheetDamage`), or against a core, a bump of the two masses (see world/junk); two ships ramming each other, each feeling half the closing speed, hurt by where they are struck (`RAM_ZONE`) |
| `blow.ts` | The spin a blow gives a hull: the hull a rod, the blow landing along it by where it comes from, its lever and the ram's impulse turning it (a shove's spin) |
| `models.ts` | The interceptors' low-poly hulls, built in code, each in coats of its own wear (see `hullSurface` in `view/surfaces.ts`: the human interceptor's paint and its ribbed fins, the alien skin of scales under a film that plays in colors with its faintly glowing rims and outline, the Sovereign's flush ivory plates without a rivet and its engraved gold; the Sovereign's hull number painted on as a decal at one place on both flanks of its nose, see `numberTexture` in `view/lettering.ts`) under a clear lacquer, shaded round with hard edges, their relief a normal map; the hull's shader shows bare steel where the paint chipped off and takes each part's finish (soot is dull) from its color; open drive bells, heat-tinted and sooty inside, with cooling rings; the light strips of the neon trim fitted along their flanks and across their sterns, hidden until worn, and the glowing skin over their radiators (ribs and vents, side blades, delta wings), hidden while cool; the four clusters of maneuvering thrusters where each hull carries them (`THRUSTER_LAYOUTS`), beveled housings with open bells; the campaign's scrap hull Lot 7731 (`scrapInterceptor`, ship type `magpie`, solo only): the human interceptor's paint worn to the metal and rusted, the yard's stencils, no gun under the nose, no tournament stripes, its left strut and fin gone, and once Pim has welded its thrusters (`Perks.patched`) a band of other steel welded on crooked near both ends of every thruster housing, its beads heat-tinted; and once Pim has ground the depot's grey off (`Perks.stripped`, Scout Two) the scout's old livery on both flanks, an off-white band chipped to the metal over a dark blue stripe, the five stars of the united Earth's emblem and PAAA 01 and S2 in letters built from bars (`SCOUT_LIVERY`, `letteringTexture` in `view/lettering.ts`); and once its left flank is closed (`Perks.bareFlank` false, Armor Panels) the right flank's strut and plate mirrored onto it in an olive of another depot, a band of fresh weld round the plate; and from chapter 13 on, and as the tournament's Magpie, its last stage (`Perks.magpie`, `magpieHull`, `showMagpie`): the scout's long nose, the sleeper pod's hump with a sensor mast on it, both struts and fins, the gun, brushed by hand in matte black and chalk white (`brushed` in the wear), flip paint along the fins' tops and the plates' leading edges (iridescence), the bulkhead's vents straw to blue, a neon magpie in sun orange on each fin and the Loose's knot on each flank of the pod |
| `thrusters.ts` | The maneuvering thrusters as built: four clusters of four nozzles at the hull's corners; which nozzles fire for a push (one side together, fore and aft balanced) and which couples for a turn (opposite corners, opposite ways), for the puffs on screen and the handbook |
| `lift.test.ts` | The lift in air: 15 g at the deck at the speed limit, by the density and the speed squared, by the slip as a plate's, trimmed to 20 g; its drag by the lift squared; the grip and the hardest turn tighter in air, with the autopilot and in free drift; a turn at full yaw tighter at speed under Jupiter's deck than in vacuum |
| `thrusters.test.ts` | Every hull's thrusters push it every way without turning it and turn it about every axis either way without moving it; a slide left puffs from the right flank |
| `bots.ts` | The bot AI: where to fly, dogfights, aiming with spread (wider under g-force strain), keeping inside an arena's edge of its own over a world (across the ground, under its ceiling, roaming low under it), keeping clear of rock along the tube its hull sweeps (a racer's, and a bot's over a world fought over, never counting a world's ground), of other ships and of the rings' housings, shoving a rival into rock, a hand that eases commands in, the timing of a race's start, the skills of each bot (a mission names them as one number or each its own, `Skill`); it knows only what a Host's sensors show, in a planet's air the haze, the safe speed and the heat alarm too, and keeps under the one and climbs out on the other; near a massive body it burns away once its escape gauge reads 30 %; friendly fire: no shot or torpedo while a ship of its own side is in the line of fire (`allyInLine`), a nova core never with one of its own side near, among rivals, at the battleship's parts, on less the longer it holds it (`novaWorth`); in a race the way its daring takes at a track's branch (`RISKY_DARING`) and its boost on a straight with the drive's heat to spare (`wantsBoost`, kept a while, `boostUntil`) |
| `bots.test.ts` | Bots fly by the Hosts' rules: their commands stay within a Host's stick, drive and thrusters and never move the ship themselves, no target behind rock, beyond sight or in a storm, a thief chased but not fired at unseen, the hull kept clear of a rock the line of flight only just misses, hunters read from noses and only within the bot's own sight, the even skills of a made-up name (also one every object inherits), the start's timing, the skill curve (the better, the quicker and closer, but never faster than a quick Host nor exact); in Jupiter's air under the safe speed without ever burning, climbing out on the heat alarm, a ship found by radar beyond the eye but not fired at, one lost that dived under the deck; near the black hole never falling in, neither for a probe on its far side nor out of a dive at the limit; the clone's strain and the nose on a ship as the sensors see it |
| `driveheat.ts` | The overdrive as a race's boost: its 8 s recharge, the drive's heat gauge (40 a shove, shedding 1 a second, `DriveHeat`), the burnout past 100 (6 s dark, 20 hull lost, see `Ship.burnOut`) |
| `driveheat.test.ts` | Two boosts as close as the recharge lets them are safe and a third burns out; the gauge sheds by itself; a bot boosts only on a straight, its nose on it, and never into a burnout (`wantsBoost`) |
| `tints.ts` | The colors ships are marked in: the palette of neon tints a Host picks from (kept in the browser), each bot's own, the trophies' own (the Sovereign's ivory and gold, the Magpie's chalk white and blue-green shimmer: `TROPHY_MARKS`), how two Hosts with the same choice are told apart, the side's color in Capture the Flag |
| `tints.test.ts` | The palette: bright, clear of the HUD's azure, the sides' colors, red and each other; every bot its own tint; the tie rule between Hosts |

Bots fly by the same rules as Hosts and know only what a Host's sensors show; a new ability of a ship is given to bots through their controls, never by setting their state. Bots are tuned with the balance runs (`npm run balance`, see `e2e/`): run them after every change here.
