The Doctrine of Flight

Out there nothing slows you down. The main drive pushes along the nose with {drive} g: the speed limit of {limit} m/s is two seconds of burn away, and so is a stop. Let go, and you drift forever.

The autopilot

Every round starts with the autopilot on. Point where you want to go: the flight computer flies the path there with drive and thrusters, and the nose follows. Full stick is the hardest turn the ship can fly, at the speed limit a circle of about {turn} m radius. W pushes along the path; let go of the stick and you fly straight on.

Free drift

T hands you the nose and nothing else. The ship slides on along its course and the stick only turns the nose: look back, shoot sideways, drift past a rival with your gun on it. The prograde marker shows where you are really going.

The quick switch

The pros switch all the time: autopilot to get somewhere, free drift for the shot, autopilot again before the next rock. The choice outlives a clone, so read the HUD: AUTOPILOT, or [ FREE DRIFT ] in brighter letters.

Flip and burn

To brake, turn the drive into the flight and burn. Hold S and the flight computer does both until you are at rest. By hand, hold X: the ship turns head over, and W fires once the nose points back. The flip alone changes nothing: you fly on backwards, gun first, which is how you shoot at whatever chases you.

The dodge

Tap a thruster twice: every thruster kicks the ship {dodge} m/s that way, more than a ship's length out of the line of fire, ready again after {dodgeRecharge} s. A laser hits the instant it fires, so nobody outruns a beam; a dodge beats the aim.

The overdrive

One shove of {overdrive} m/s along the nose, past the speed limit. It leaves your clone at the edge of blacking out and the main drive dead for {overdriveHeat} s, while thrusters and laser keep working, and recharges for {overdriveRecharge} s. Fire it across an attacker's line of fire, never straight away from a ship on your tail: that only makes you a farther target.

Glory at Full Burn

{gates} gates, {laps} laps, around the field's center through the outer rocks. Every gate scores {gatePoints} points, first place at the finish {firstPlace}, and less for every place after. Lasers stay on: a kill scores {raceKill}.

The line

The dashed ribbon is the ideal line: through the center of every gate, around rock as it lies right now. The farther you stray, the brighter it glows, so you find it again under fire.

The X

The X marks where your flight, held as it is, crosses the next gate's plane. Azure: you will pass through. Orange: you will miss. Steer the X into the ring. A missed gate has to be flown again, from either side.

Brake zones

Where the line ahead bends tighter than you can turn at your speed, it glows orange. Ease off or brake before the orange, not in it. The bends at the gates can be taken at the speed limit; the bends around rock are the trap.

The coils

Every gate is a coil {gateRadius} m in radius. Through its inner third, within {core} m of the center, it throws you on {boost} m/s faster, even past the speed limit. Off-center it gives nothing, and the housing is solid: hitting it is a crash like rock. The clean line is the fast one.

The start

The clamps hold every ship through the countdown. Light the drive within {early} s before BURN! or {late} s after it: a perfect start, a shove of {startShove} m/s. Earlier, and the drive stalls for {stall} s.

The probes on the track

A race's probes are sealed, and luck favors the back of the field: torpedoes, overdrives and the nova cores go to the last third, plating and repairs to the leaders.

Victory Through Superior Position

Armor and the drive

There are no shields. Nose and flanks are armor: a beam only scorches it, {armor} of {hull} hull points. From behind, within {driveCone}° of the axis, a beam hits the main drive: each hit costs a fifth of its thrust, and the fifth sends the reactor critical. Five hits from behind win the fight.

So a fight is won by position: get behind the other ship, and keep your armored nose to anyone behind you. The crosshair glows orange only over a drive. ON YOUR TAIL means: turn your nose to that ship, now.

Heat and bursts

The laser needs no ammunition, only cooling. Each shot adds {heatShot} % heat: a cool laser fires a burst of {burstSun} in sunlight, {burstShade} in a rock's shadow. Overheated, it locks until it has cooled to {cooled} %. Fire short bursts while the crosshair glows, and cool off in the shade (☾ beside the heat gauge).

Cover

Rock stops everything: beams, sensors and torpedo seekers. A ship behind a rock vanishes from the HUD, and the bots cannot see it either. In a rock's shadow your laser also cools more than twice as fast.

Torpedoes

{torpedoes} per clone. A torpedo locks on the ship closest to your nose within {lockAngle}° and {lockRange} km: keep the target near the crosshair until the lock holds, then launch. Armor does not stop it: {torpedoDamage} hull points, whatever side it reaches. Two kill.

TORPEDO INBOUND: shoot it down (a small target, {torpedoShot} points), or break its lock. A seeker that loses sight behind a rock or a hull flies straight on for good.

Or outfly it. A torpedo flies at up to {torpedoSpeed} m/s, more than your {limit} m/s: running straight away only buys time, and only an overdrive outruns it, for a moment. But it turns with {torpedoTurn} g, you with {grip}. Nicole counts it down (a thousand, five hundred, three hundred, two hundred, a hundred meters) and calls “Break!” about {torpedoBreak} s before it hits: turn hard across its path now, or dodge a moment later, and it overshoots. Hold the turn, and it comes round again; jink one way, then the other, and it burns out after {torpedoLife} s.

The hook

A harpoon flies along the nose and bites the first rock or ship within {hookRange} km. Held, the winch reels in at {hookPull} g, and the cable never gets longer: a ship flying past its rock swings around it, faster the more cable comes in. Let go, and you fly off along the swing like a stone from a sling. Ready again after {hookReady} s.

Hooked to a ship, the winch pulls you both together while each drive pulls against it: a tug of war. The hook does not take a sponsor core; a core is taken by flying close to it.

Probes

From {probeFirst} s into a round a sponsor probe shows up every {probeEvery} s, each carrying one thing. Fly within {probeReach} m of it to take it. Its color says what it holds:

  • Twin laser (pink): twice as fast, half the heat per shot, for the clone's life.
  • Plating (blue): two hits into the drive bounce off, or one torpedo.
  • Torpedoes (orange): two more.
  • Overdrive (yellow): charged at once.
  • Repair (green): hull, drive and thrusters as new.
  • Nova core (white): see below.

A dead clone's twin laser, plating and nova core fly out again as probes, for whoever gets there first.

The nova core

One probe in fourteen. Your next press of fire is no laser: the core goes off and destroys every other ship within {nova} m, each a double kill. Your clone burns up with it, free of charge: no death, no ad. So fly into the crowd first, then fire. Within {novaPanic} km of a holder the siren tells everyone else to run.

Unsportsmanlike Conduct, Approved

The Ministry calls it entertainment, and the rules allow all of it.

Shoves and knockouts

A ship struck by another at {shove} m/s or more is shoved, and if it crashes within {knockout} s (into rock, a gate's housing, the battleship's hull, the flare or deep space), the shover gets the kill: a knockout. Your tool is the dodge: kicked into a ship alongside, it throws that ship off at about {shoveSpeed} m/s. The shove barely scratches; the rock beside the line does the killing. At a race's speed a ship shoved off its line meets the housing ahead at full speed.

Ramming

Below {bump} m/s two ships only bump. Faster, it is a ram, and its blow grows with the square of the speed. Where it lands decides: the armored nose takes {ramNose} % of the blow, a flank {ramFlank} %, an open drive all of it. Ram with your nose into their drive, never the other way round. A ram that kills is your kill, a kamikaze too.

The sling

Hook a rock, swing around it, let go: you leave faster than you came, on a line nobody expects. Let go at the wrong moment, and the same sling throws you into the next rock.

On the rope

A rival on your cable cannot get away from you while its drive fights your winch. Reel it in and give it the laser, or a torpedo, from close range.

The grill

A main drive burning hard throws plasma {plume} m behind it. Whoever tails you too closely gets grilled, and a clone that burns up counts as your kill. Overtake beside a plume, never through it.

Deathmatch: The Purest Show

Five minutes, everyone against everyone. A kill scores {kill}, a rock {rock1}, its fragments {rock2} and theirs {rock3}, a close call past a rock {closeCall}. The best score wins.

The bots hunt the nearest ship they see, at most two on the same one. Keep moving: steady flight is easy to lead, jinking with the thrusters makes them miss. A ship crossing your nose fast up close: switch to free drift, slide on, and turn the nose onto it.

Last Clone Standing: Run from the Sun

{clones} clones each; when your last one dies, you are out until the next round. From the first minute a solar flare eats into the arena from the sun's side at {flareSpeed} m/s, and at four minutes it holds, leaving a pocket on the far side about {pocket} km deep, with the field's outermost rocks as cover.

In the plasma a ship burns, {flareHeat} heat and {flareBurn} hull a second; behind a rock, away from the sun, a quarter of that. A rock buys time, not safety. The HUD marks the pocket: get there early, before the crowd. Whoever has the most clones left at the end wins, then whoever held out longest.

Capture the Flag: For Sponsor and Side

Orange against Blue, each side with a ring for a base and its sponsor core inside. Fly within {grab} m of the other side's core and it is yours in tow ({grabPoints} points), on a cable behind and below you. Bring it into your own ring while your own core is at home: a capture, {capturePoints} points. {captures} captures win at once.

A carrier that dies drops the core where it was. Touch your own core adrift to send it home ({returnPoints} points); after {adrift} s adrift it goes home by itself. Nothing hurts your own side, except a drive plume and a ram. Guard with a few, raid with the rest, and never fly straight at a core under the guards' guns: swing out wide and dive through it already heading home.

The President's Fleet: Sink His Pride

Everyone on one side, against the President's battleship. It comes in {arrival} s into the round and circles the field. Its hull takes no harm and stops every beam, so the side away from a battery is cover. Its parts break: a battery after {battery} laser hits, a drive after {driveHits}, the reactor after {reactor}. A torpedo counts {torpedoHits} hits on every part near where it goes off.

Take out the batteries first: every {batteryPeriod} s each fires at the nearest ship it sees within {batteryRange} km, and speed spoils its aim. With half its batteries gone it turns its stern on the nearest pilot and lights its drives: stay out of that plume. With every battery gone the reactor opens. When it breaks, get clear: four seconds later the ship goes up and takes everything within {blast} km. Sinking it scores {sink} for everyone; if the five minutes run out first, the President wins.

Seven Arenas, One Glorious Death

  • The Belt. The home of the Games: forty huge rocks, cover everywhere.
  • Rings of Kronos. Ice in a thin sheet, open above and below. Ice breaks easier, and the weaker sun lets lasers cool a little faster.
  • Kessler Field. Station debris on crossing orbits, all at {kesslerOrbit} m/s: your cover moves. The station's core in the middle never breaks.
  • Gas Giant. Its pull, almost a g, drags everything towards the clouds below, and the clouds burn up whatever touches them. Never drift for long.
  • Corona. In the sun's glare the radiators shed only about a third of their heat: fight from the shadows. Every {stormEvery} s a solar storm blinds the sensors for {stormLength} s.
  • Event Horizon. A black hole in the middle. Within about {noReturn} m no drive gets out; watch the PULL gauge and burn away early.
  • The Derelict. A broken world ship and nothing else: no rocks, only its hull to hide behind, and lasers only scorch it.

Hazards and Alarms

Below the crosshair the flight computer names the worst danger: CAUTION in azure, WARNING in orange, CRITICAL in red with a siren. One line per danger, with an arrow to where it comes from and a countdown where it has one.

Impact

IMPACT means your course hits something sooner than you could flip and brake. Turn or brake now. Below {impactSafe} m/s a rock only scratches the paint; at about {impactKill} m/s it wrecks the ship.

Comets

COMET INBOUND comes {cometWarning} s before a comet reaches the arena. Its head destroys whatever it meets. The shower in its tail is the real danger: every piece strikes like a laser beam. Get into the lee of a rock, or turn your armored nose into the shower.

Gravity wells

Near the black hole and over the gas giant the PULL gauge shows how much of your drive it would take to get out: CAUTION from {wellCaution} %, WARNING from {wellWarning} %, CRITICAL from {wellCritical} %, and when it would take all of it, NO RETURN. Burn away early: falling, the gauge climbs fast.

The arena's edge

Space has no walls, but the Games do. More than {edge} km from the center the clock runs: {grace} s to get back, or the clone is lost in deep space. Outside, the whole edge glows orange, so the way home is always in sight.

Hull failing

At {hullCritical} hull points or less: HULL FAILING. One more hit and you are dead. Find cover, or a repair probe.

Points, Coins and the Hangar

A kill {kill} (in a race {raceKill}), rocks {rock1}, {rock2} and {rock3}, a close call {closeCall}, a gate {gatePoints}, first place in a race {firstPlace}. Every point is paid out as one Solar Coin at the round's end; training is free and pays nothing.

In the hangar the coins buy upgrades for both interceptors, three steps each: radiators, thrusters, main drive, torpedo tubes, plating, and the looks (neon trim, drive flame). Online every upgrade counts for one step at most, so newcomers meet veterans almost as equals; the looks count in full. Complete both interceptors, and the President gives you the Sovereign: a trophy, not a better ship.

Honor among Hosts

The Games run without a central server. The Hosts' browsers build the tournament's network among themselves and agree on what happens by consensus.

They are meant to be flown with friends. Whoever cheats, cheats their friends. If that is fun for you, have fun while it lasts: the other Hosts will cast you out.

Whoever cheats their friends is an asshole. Period.

And yes, we know what DevTools are. Go ahead and edit your localStorage. Out there are Hosts who fly the flight school themselves and earn their Solar Coins round by round, to build the perfect ship the honest way. Online the upgrades count only up to their cap anyway.
