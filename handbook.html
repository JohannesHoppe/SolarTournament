Welcome to the Presidential Games

Congratulations, Host! You have been chosen to serve the United Solar States in the greatest show of all time. You stay safe at home, and a clone printed from you flies your ship: radio links lag, and out there only a mind in the cockpit is fast enough. Over the live link, everything your clone lives through streams into your memory as it happens.*

*When your clone dies, you live through its death, the pain included. The next clone boots, and you remember. The soul rots a little more each time.

The United Solar States are led by the Orange President, for a thousand glorious years and counting. Whenever he dies, a fresh clone of him boots, as brilliant as ever. To the people of the system he gives his Presidential Games, staged by the Ministry of War among the asteroids and broadcast to every planet.*

*Every transfer copies his dementia along with him and makes it worse; nobody can predict him any more. The Games keep the people entertained, and quiet.

Your President protects you. Against the Rockborn, the miners of the belt who keep trying to come back to Earth, he is building the Solar Wall, the biggest wall in the history of walls, and Mars is going to pay for it. Against the “aliens”, you protect yourself: in the Games you may shoot them down in person.*

*The Rockborn mine the very rocks you fight among, and Earth deports them as soon as they come home. The “aliens” are human, changed beyond recognition by generations in deep space; calling them aliens makes it easier to cheer when they die. Mars has not agreed to pay.

Your interceptor is a proud veteran from the Ministry's depots, with centuries of service behind it. The President's own battleship shines in gold, fresh from the newest dock, refitted to his own designs: the greatest ship ever built. When he wants blood, he sends it into the ring.*

*Ships are never printed. Yours is an old fighter from the depots of centuries of war, patched and repainted more often than anyone counts, cheaper to burn up in a show than to scrap. The golden carrier was paid for by abolishing all health care: who needs doctors when clones are printed by the dozen?

A new clone costs you nothing but a moment of attention: our generous sponsors pay for it. The tournament radio drives every round with hard beats, its announcer honors every fallen pilot on air (“an excellent death”), and in your cockpit Nicole, your flight computer, keeps you company: “Do not look so sad, darling. Bodies are cheap. You are not.”*

*Want another clone? Make the sponsor happy. Nicole is a product of the Ministry, made to keep Hosts flying into their deaths gladly. One day, she promises, you will meet her for real. There is always one more round to go first.

The Doctrine of Flight

Out there nothing slows you down. The main drive pushes along the nose with {drive} g: the speed limit of {limit} m/s is two seconds of burn away, and so is a stop. Let go, and you drift forever.

The maneuvering thrusters

Besides the main drive your ship carries {nozzles} small nozzles: {quads} clusters of {quadNozzles}, one at each corner of the hull, fore and aft on both flanks. Each nozzle of a cluster points another way: out from the flank, up, down, and along the ship. Gas leaving a nozzle pushes the ship the other way.

Nozzles on one side firing together move the ship without turning it: both clusters of the right flank fire, and the ship slides left, with up to {thrust} g. Two nozzles at opposite corners pushing opposite ways turn the ship about its center without moving it, up to {turnRate}° a second, reached in {turnReach} s; the other two stop the turn, because out there nothing else does. The flight computer picks the nozzles for you. Only the main drive pushes harder, and only forward.

Free drift

Every round starts in free drift, the way a real spaceship flies. The ship slides on along its course and steering only turns the nose: look back, shoot sideways, drift past a rival with your gun on it. The crosshair is where you shoot, the Course marker where you really go: a faint dashed line joins the two, and when the marker slips off screen an arrow at the edge points to it.

You steer your flight with the drive: it pushes along the nose. To bend your course, put the nose between the Course marker and where you want to go, and burn; the marker slides towards the crosshair, and when it sits where you want it, let go. To slow down for a bend, nose against the flight and burn. That is how races are won, on the line at full speed.

Wherever the nose points, the HUD shows where you move: faint blue streaks flow out of the Course marker the way the world rushes past, faster the faster you go. Sliding sideways, they sweep across the screen; flying backwards, they stream into the Brake marker.

The autopilot

T switches on the autopilot: the ship flies where it points. Turn the nose, and the flight computer brings your flight round behind it with thrusters and drive; the crosshair is where you fly and where you shoot. At speed the flight follows slowly, so the nose waits for it: at the speed limit it leads by at most {lead}°, and the flight is back within {onNose}° of the nose {settle} s after you stop turning. The hardest turn at the speed limit is a circle of about {turn} m radius, and the drive only helps while you hold it, or at the limit: coasting below it, a hard turn costs speed. It is an aid for when your head is busy with something else; in a fight it costs you the shot sideways. The choice outlives a clone, so read the HUD: AUTOPILOT, or [ FREE DRIFT ] in brighter letters.

Flip, brake and return

Coasting never slows you down: in space only a burn against the flight brakes. The flip (X) turns the nose against the flight and holds it there while you hold it. With W you burn as long as you hold it: let go early and keep some speed, hold on past zero and you speed up backwards. The flip alone changes nothing: you fly on backwards, gun first, which is how you shoot at whatever chases you.

The brake (S) is a switch. Press it, and it remembers where you look, flips the ship and burns with the full drive, taking out the drift sideways too. Press it again when you like your speed, and it turns you back to exactly where you looked, nose and roll alike; at rest it does that by itself. A dotted ring marks the target. Meanwhile you may steer, to shoot a chaser, say: the drive only burns while the nose points roughly against the flight, and once you let go the brake turns it back. W breaks the maneuver off at once. That is the racer's move: brake for a bend, and the nose is back on the line.

The dodge

Tap a thruster twice: every thruster kicks the ship {dodge} m/s that way, more than a ship's length out of the line of fire, ready again after {dodgeRecharge} s. A laser hits the instant it fires, so nobody outruns a beam; a dodge beats the aim.

The overdrive

One shove of {overdrive} m/s past the speed limit, and one rule for it: it puts you onto the crosshair's line. A rival or a torpedo the aim assist holds, else the first thing the laser would hit (rock, wreckage, the battleship), else empty space ahead: the shove takes out the drift that would carry you past it and closes on it straight. Into a drive or a flank that kills, and your armored nose holds; into rock it kills you. It leaves your clone at the edge of blacking out and the main drive dead for {overdriveHeat} s, while thrusters and laser keep working, and recharges for {overdriveRecharge} s. Fire it across an attacker's line of fire, never straight away from a ship on your tail: that only makes you a farther target.

The hover field

Over the ground of a world (a moon, a planet, a planetoid such as Psyche) your hover field holds you {hoverHeight} m up: it pushes against the world's gravity, the harder the closer you come. Touching the ground never hurts: the hull glides over it and only scrapes off a little speed. With the autopilot you ride the ground's shape over crests and hollows; point the nose up to climb away. The field needs a world's pull: in open space and against a rock it does nothing, and rock stays deadly.

Under your height the flight computer shows what decides how hard you can turn, only where it acts: GRAVITY, the pull you are in, in g; AIR, its density as a share of the density under the cloud deck; HOVER, your height over the ground, orange while the hull scrapes it; and GRIP, the hardest turn you can fly now, in g.

Glory at Full Burn

{gates} gates, {laps} laps, on a circuit each arena has of its own: long straights for the drive, bends wide enough to take at full speed. Every gate scores {gatePoints} points, first place at the finish {firstPlace}, and less for every place after. Lasers stay on: a kill scores {raceKill}.

The line

The dashed ribbon is the ideal line: through the center of every gate, around rock as it lies right now. The farther you stray, the brighter it glows, so you find it again under fire.

The X

The X marks where your flight, held as it is, crosses the next gate's plane. Azure: you will pass through. Orange: you will miss. Steer the X into the ring.

One gate a lap may be left out. The next gate glows orange, the one after it blue, and both count: fly through the blue one, and the orange one is skipped, without its points. Miss a gate, and the line takes you on to the next. Your skip for that lap is spent; a second missed gate has to be flown again, from either side. The pink gate can never be left out.

Brake zones

Where the line ahead bends tighter than you can turn at your speed, it glows orange: brake before the orange, not in it. Hold the drive with your nose on the line, and you take every bend at the speed limit; only rock on the move bends it tighter.

The coils

Every gate is a coil {gateRadius} m in radius. Through its inner third, within {core} m of the center, it throws you on {boost} m/s faster, even past the speed limit. Off-center it gives nothing, and the housing is solid: hitting it is a crash like rock. The clean line is the fast one.

The start

The clamps hold every ship through the countdown. Light the drive within {early} s before BURN! or {late} s after it: a perfect start, a shove of {startShove} m/s. Earlier, and the drive stalls for {stall} s.

Races over a world

Some races run over a world instead of round a loop: one run of {trackGates} gates from the start to the finish, low over its ground. Its gates stand on the ground, their lower housing sunk into it: riding your hover field low you fly through the opening, through the coil if you keep to its middle. The ground and its walls never hurt you; what stands on the ground does. A crash costs seconds, never the race: your next clone is back after {trackRespawn} s at the last gate you passed, already flying along the track at {trackSpeed} m/s, without a sponsor break. One gate a race may be left out, never the start nor the finish.

Two ways

Where a track splits into two ways that join again, every gate between stands on both, and either counts: both glow as your next gate. One way is shorter and riskier (through a gorge between sheer walls, a bend tighter than you turn in free drift at the limit), the other longer and safer. Your racing line and the gate's mark follow the way your flight heads for at the split, and from a gate on the way you passed it on; a crash puts you back on that way.

The boost

In a race the overdrive is a boost: it recharges in {boostRecharge} s, your drive burns on after it, and it never takes you past {boostTop} m/s. But every shove heats the drive by {boostHeat} on a gauge of its own, which sheds only {boostShed} a second; past {burnoutHeat} the drive burns out, dark for {burnoutSeconds} s, and the hull loses {burnoutDamage}. Two shoves as close together as the recharge lets them are safe, a third that soon burns: watch the gauge turn orange, wait until it is down, and save your last shove for the straight before the finish. Boost out of a bend, nose on the line, never into one.

The probes on the track

A race's probes are sealed, and luck favors the back of the field: torpedoes, overdrives and the nova cores go to the last third, plating and repairs to the leaders.

Victory Through Superior Position

Armor and the drive

There are no shields. Nose and flanks are armor: a beam only scorches it, {armor} of {hull} hull points. From behind, within {driveCone}° of the axis, a beam hits the main drive: each hit costs a fifth of its thrust, and the fifth sends the reactor critical. Five hits from behind win the fight.

Nanobots, gear from the hangar, mend your drive and thrusters {nanoWait} s after the last blow to them, whole again in {nanoSeconds} s; the hull stays as it is. So a hurt drive is no reason to give up: break off, take cover behind a rock, and come back at full thrust.

So a fight is won by position: get behind the other ship, and keep your armored nose to anyone behind you. The crosshair glows orange only over a drive. ON YOUR TAIL means: turn your nose to that ship, now.

Whoever hit you in the last few seconds carries a red warning sign on its mark, the latest one pulsing, and when it hit you from out of sight, Nicole names it. Turn round, and you know whom to take on.

Getting behind a bot is a matter of drift: your flight slides on, and your nose stays on its drive. With the autopilot on, your flight would follow your nose and carry you past it.

Heat and bursts

The laser needs no ammunition, only cooling. Each shot adds {heatShot} % heat: a cool laser fires a burst of {burstSun} in sunlight, {burstShade} in a rock's shadow. Overheated, it locks until it has cooled to {cooled} %. Fire short bursts while the crosshair glows, and cool off in the shade (☾ beside the heat gauge).

Hot radiators glow for everyone to see: dull red, then orange-red as the laser nears its lock. A rival glowing bright is about to fall silent; while yours glow, every rival knows the same of you.

Cover

Rock stops everything: beams, sensors and torpedo seekers. A ship behind a rock vanishes from the HUD, and the bots cannot see it either. In a rock's shadow your laser also cools more than twice as fast.

Not every rock lasts. A rock's fragments stay all round, but their fragments, the smallest pieces, crumble to dust {crumble} s after they break off, and a comet's head grinds every fragment in its way to dust. Cover behind a small piece is cover for a moment.

Ships stop beams too. A beam ends at the first hull on its line, and it cannot tell friend from foe: a ship of your own side in your line of fire takes the shot, a torpedo goes off at the first hull it meets, and a nova core takes your side with everyone else. A kill of your own side costs you {kill} points, a nova's twice that, and the announcer tells the whole system what you are. Your HUD marks your own side with a ring where a rival has a square, and while your nose is on one of them the crosshair is crossed out over FRIEND. Fire past your side, never through it.

Silent running

A burning drive and a firing laser glow on every sensor. Leave your drive cold and your trigger alone for {silentAfter} s, and you run silent: the radar, the HUD's marks, a torpedo's lock and the bots find you only within {silentRange} km. Your HUD says SILENT RUNNING. Drift out of a fight, let them lose you, and come back where they do not look. The eye still sees you, and the first burn or shot gives you away.

Torpedoes

{torpedoes} per ship, more with the hangar's tubes. A torpedo locks on the ship closest to your nose within {lockAngle}° and {lockRange} km: keep the target near the crosshair until the lock holds, then launch. Armor does not stop it: {torpedoDamage} hull points, whatever side it reaches. Two kill.

TORPEDO INBOUND: shoot it down (a small target, {torpedoShot} points), or break its lock. A seeker that loses sight behind a rock or a hull flies straight on for good.

A rival's torpedo in sight within {lockRange} km carries a hollow triangle in its shooter's color; without it, it is a dot of a few pixels. The aim assist takes it as it takes a ship: bring it near the crosshair and the nose is pulled onto it, the triangle turns orange, and the crosshair glows while a shot would set it off.

Or outfly it. A torpedo flies at up to {torpedoSpeed} m/s, more than your {limit} m/s: running straight away only buys time, and only an overdrive outruns it, for a moment. But it turns with {torpedoTurn} g, you with {grip}. Nicole counts it down (a thousand, five hundred, three hundred, two hundred, a hundred meters) and calls “Break!” about {torpedoBreak} s before it hits: turn hard across its path now, or dodge a moment later, and it overshoots. Hold the turn, and it comes round again; jink one way, then the other, and it burns out after {torpedoBurn} s.

A torpedo on your hook is a bomb on a string. It weighs {torpedoMass} t, and its motor still pushes for its target at {torpedoThrust} g, but your drive drags it wherever you go; after {torpedoBurn} s it is just a mass on the rope. Its fuse goes off on every ship, yours too: swing it into a rival, or let go to fling it. While you hold it, and {fling} s after, its kill is yours. The HUD says TORPEDO ON THE LINE.

The hook

A harpoon flies along the nose and bites the first rock, ship, sponsor probe or torpedo within {hookRange} km; a probe it bites comes in on the cable and is yours when it arrives, unless another ship flies through it first. While it holds, the winch reels in at {hookPull} g, and the cable never gets longer: a ship flying past its rock swings around it, faster the more cable comes in. Press the hook again to let go, and you fly off along the swing like a stone from a sling. Ready again after {hookReady} s. The hook is gear: buy it in the hangar; the flight school lends you one for its drills.

The cable only pulls, and every jerk is shared by mass: a rock is millions of times your {shipMass} t and stays where it is, a rival of your mass is pulled as hard as you. Hooked to a ship, both drives pull against the rope: a tug of war the stronger drive wins, and an overdrive's shove yanks the other along. In Capture the Flag the hook takes a sponsor core too.

A cable never passes through anything solid: it wraps around it. When rock, ice, junk or a hull comes between you and what you hooked, the cable lies on its edge and you swing around that edge; swing back, and it comes off again. Let go before it winds onto the rock: the winch keeps you clear of it by paying out cable, and that eats your swing. It snaps on a planet's or a black hole's body, on the battleship's hull, on a rock that sweeps right through it, and once it has wound a whole turn round a rock.

Probes

From {probeFirst} s into a round a sponsor probe shows up every {probeEvery} s, each carrying one thing. Fly within {probeReach} m of it to take it. Its color says what it holds:

  • Twin laser (pink): twice as fast, half the heat per shot, for the clone's life.
  • Plating (blue): two hits into the drive bounce off, or one torpedo.
  • Torpedoes (orange): {probeTorpedoes} more.
  • Overdrive (yellow): charged at once.
  • Repair (green): hull, drive and thrusters as new.
  • Nova core (white): see below.
  • Decoys (violet): two hot lures, held up to three. Drop one off the stern with its key, and the torpedo homing on you nearest to it turns to it and goes off there: one decoy, one torpedo. Drop it late, when the torpedo is near, and against a salvo one for every torpedo. A clonk through the hull and a jolt tell you it is away.

A dead clone's twin laser, plating and nova core fly out again as probes, for whoever gets there first.

The nova core

About one probe in eight. Your next press of fire is no laser: the core goes off and destroys every other ship within {nova} m that it sees, your own side's too, each rival a double kill; a rock between it and a hull shields that hull, and the rock and ice close by break. Your clone burns up with it, free of charge: no death, no ad. So fly into the crowd first, then fire, where no rock covers them. Within {novaPanic} km of a holder the siren tells everyone else to run, or to get a rock between.

The self-destruct

Gear from the hangar. Hold its key for {selfDestructHold} s, and your reactor goes off: every ship within {selfDestruct} m dies with you, each a kill of yours. Your own clone dies too, a death like a crash, ad and all. The last answer to a rival on your tail or on your hook: reel it in, then hold. Not in a race, not in the flight school.

Unsportsmanlike Conduct, Approved

The Ministry calls it entertainment, and the rules allow all of it.

Shoves and knockouts

A ship struck by another at {shove} m/s or more is shoved, and if it crashes within {knockout} s (into rock, a gate's housing, the battleship's hull, the flare or deep space), the shover gets the kill: a knockout. Your tool is the dodge: kicked into a ship alongside, it throws that ship off at about {shoveSpeed} m/s. The shove dents both hulls; the rock beside the line does the killing. At a race's speed a ship shoved off its line meets the housing ahead at full speed.

Ramming

Below {bump} m/s two ships only bump. Faster, it is a ram: each of you takes the blow of half your closing speed, as from a rock. Where it lands decides: the armored nose takes {ramNose} % of the blow, a flank {ramFlank} %, an open drive {ramDrive} %. Ram with your nose into their drive, never the other way round. A ram that kills is your kill, a kamikaze too. Whatever else you hit hurts by its mass the same way: a torn plate of wreckage is a knock, a ship's hulk at speed a crash.

The sling

Hook a rock, swing around it, let go: you leave faster than you came, on a line nobody expects. Let go at the wrong moment, and the same sling throws you into the next rock.

On the rope

A rival on your cable cannot get away from you while its drive fights your winch. Reel it in and give it the laser, or a torpedo, from close range.

The grill

A main drive burning hard throws plasma {plume} m behind it. Whoever tails you too closely gets grilled, and a clone that burns up counts as your kill. Overtake beside a plume, never through it.

Deathmatch: The Purest Show

Five minutes, everyone against everyone. A kill scores {kill}, a close call past a rock {closeCall}. The laser only scorches rock: it is the sponsors', and breaks for nobody's points. The best score wins.

The bots hunt the nearest ship they see, at most two on the same one. Keep moving: steady flight is easy to lead, jinking with the thrusters makes them miss. A ship crossing your nose fast up close: switch to free drift, slide on, and turn the nose onto it.

Last Clone Standing: Run from the Sun

{clones} clones each; when your last one dies, you are out until the next round. From the first minute a solar flare eats into the arena from the sun's side at {flareSpeed} m/s, and at four minutes it holds, leaving a pocket on the far side about {pocket} km deep, with the field's outermost rocks as cover.

In the plasma a ship burns, {flareHeat} heat and {flareBurn} hull a second; behind a rock, away from the sun, a quarter of that. A rock buys time, not safety. The HUD marks the pocket: get there early, before the crowd. Whoever has the most clones left at the end wins, then whoever held out longest.

Capture the Flag: For Sponsor and Side

Orange against Blue, each side with a ring for a base and its sponsor core inside. Take the other side's core with your hook ({grabPoints} points): fly within {grab} m of it, or fire the harpoon at it from up to {hookRange} km. It then hangs on a cable of {towCable} m behind you, a trailer of {coreMass} t with a mind of its own: it swings out in turns, overtakes you when you brake and bounces off whatever it meets, harming nothing. Fly it through your own ring while your own core is at home: when the core itself passes through the ring, it is a capture, {capturePoints} points. {captures} captures win at once.

A tow cable wraps around rock as the hook's does. A carrier that dies, or whose tow cable snaps, lets the core go where it hangs, and its thrusters brake it to rest at {coreBrake} g. Take your own core adrift with your hook to send it home ({returnPoints} points); after {adrift} s adrift it goes home by itself. Your own side does not spare you: its beams, torpedoes and novas hurt you as a rival's do, and a kill of your own side costs its killer {kill} points. Guard with a few, raid with the rest, and never fly straight at a core under the guards' guns: swing out wide and dive through it already heading home, or hook it from afar.

A core is solid: a beam stops on it and a torpedo goes off at it, so a core in tow covers its carrier from behind. Shoot the carrier from the side. A core adrift that falls into a gravity well or meets a comet's head is lost, and its sponsor's spare waits at home.

The President's Fleet: Sink His Pride

Everyone on one side, against the President's battleship. It comes in {arrival} s into the round and circles the field. Its hull takes no harm and stops every beam, so the side away from a battery is cover. Its turrets, their barrels and its drive bells are as solid as its hull, and a beam that strikes them hits the battery or the drive. Its parts break: a battery after {battery} laser hits, a drive after {driveHits}, the reactor after {reactor}. A torpedo counts {torpedoHits} hits on every part near where it goes off.

A nova core is the heaviest blow it can take: every part within {novaShatter} m of the core breaks outright, and every part it reaches farther out loses a share of all the hits it takes, down to {novaEdge} % at {nova} m. Set it off over the deck between the batteries, with your own side clear of the blast. The reactor stays sealed under its dome until the batteries are gone.

Take out the batteries first: every {batteryPeriod} s each fires at the nearest ship it sees within {batteryRange} km, and speed spoils its aim. With half its batteries gone it turns its stern on the nearest pilot and lights its drives: stay out of that plume. With every battery gone the reactor opens. When it breaks, get clear: four seconds later the ship goes up and takes everything within {blast} km. Sinking it scores {sink} for everyone; if the five minutes run out first, the President wins.

Under the flight deck {hangars} hangar tunnels run straight through the hull from flank to flank, {hangarWidth} m wide and {hangarHeight} m high: dive in on one side and out the other. Inside, no battery sees you, and a chaser has to follow you through or lose you. The battleship moves at {fleetSpeed} m/s, so lead the far mouth as you lead a crossing ship, or its wall catches you.

Seven Arenas, One Glorious Death

  • The Belt. The home of the Games: forty huge rocks, cover everywhere. The Rockborn mine them: their rigs, buoys and cutters are solid steel: ships crash on them, and beams, torpedoes and hooks stop at them, so they are cover too. The dust from their drill holes does no harm, it only patters on the hull.
  • Rings of Saturn. Inside Saturn's ring: a layer of ice grains {ringLayer} m thick runs through the middle, small chunks of ice in it, a few big ones in the open space above and below. The grains do no harm, they only patter on the hull; dive through the layer to shake off a chaser, or hide among its chunks. The race course dives through it four times a lap. The sun gives a hundredth of the light it gives Earth; the flight computer's sensors brighten the picture to make up for it. Ice breaks easier, and the weaker sun lets lasers cool a little faster.

  • The Satellite Junkyard. The dead satellites, spent stages and fairings of the Orbital Freedom Network on crossing orbits, all at {junkOrbit} m/s: your cover moves. The dead relay hub in the middle never breaks. Earth glows below, out of reach.*

    Most of the junk is thin. Your armor punches straight through a solar wing, a fairing's shell or a stage's empty tank, knocking it into a slow tumble, and what it tears out dents your nose by how fast you hit it: a wing a scrape, a fairing a dent, a tank at full speed a hard hit, but no sheet ever wrecks a whole hull. What is dense still hurts: a satellite's foil-wrapped bus strikes you by its mass, a small one hard, a large one like rock, and so do a stage's engine and a stack of satellites never released. Fly through the wings and shells, round a tank when there is time, and round the cores. A torpedo still goes off on anything it touches, and a beam stops at a sheet too: the thin junk is cover for your enemy as much as for you.

    *They promised internet for everyone. Now everyone gets junk, darling.

  • Jupiter. Its cloud deck runs through the middle of the arena: above it open space, below it the air, thicker the deeper you go, and its pull, almost a g, draws everything down into it. Half the fight is in the clouds (see In Jupiter's clouds).
  • Corona. In the sun's glare the radiators shed only about half their heat: fight from the shadows. Every {stormEvery} s a solar storm blinds the sensors for {stormLength} s.
  • Event Horizon. A black hole in the middle. Within about {noReturn} m no drive gets out; watch the PULL gauge and burn away early, or fall around it in an orbit (see Orbits).

  • The Derelict. A broken world ship and nothing else: no rocks, only its hull to hide behind, and lasers only scorch it. The mist of ice crystals still escaping at the break does no harm, it only patters on the hull. Its dead drift there, frozen; nothing you fire touches them.*

    *They set out together for the stars. Now they watch you die over their grave, darling. Mind them as you pass.

Races are flown in five of the seven: the Corona and the Event Horizon are fought in, never raced. On the solo plate a race shows the worlds raced over first, marked RACE WORLD.

The Crack. Races alone fly over this world: a rift in Europa's ice, its floor some {riftFloor} m wide, its walls climbing {riftDepth} m in terraces, under a pull of {crackGravity} g, Jupiter low ahead. The track runs down the rift and under the slabs of ice fallen across it, rides up out of it through a field of chaos, tilted rafts of ice, drops into the rift again past the science station and on through its narrows, rises out once more and runs back over the double ridges to the finish, {trackLength} km in all (see Races over a world). Over a ridge's crest the ground falls away under you for a hop of a few seconds.

It splits twice (see Two ways): where the rift swings out in a wide bow, follow it, or fly straight on through the gorge, {gorgeDepth} m deep between sheer walls; at the bend swing round wide, or turn early and tight.

The Throat. Races alone fly over this world too: the winding strait of Titan's methane sea, low under the deck of its orange haze, under a pull of {throatGravity} g, in an air clear enough to see the next bend coming some {throatSight} km off. Its Esses and the Kraken's Elbow tighten to {throatBend} m, where at {limit} m/s even the autopilot turns no tighter than {vacuumTurn} m in a vacuum; the air over the water lifts you round them at full speed, down to {throatCarve} m with the autopilot, so keep the nose off the flight and carve. Past the Elbow the strait loops round the Mesa, and a canyon cuts straight through it, some {throatShortcut} m shorter, narrow and winding between walls of rock: the bolder way. The track runs {throatLength} km from the northern basin to the southern one, and Titan's air clogs no radiators, so racing at the limit never burns your hull.

Worlds fought over. Every other deathmatch of a tournament is fought low over a world, under its pull, its ground below and the arena's edge above. Your clone is printed on the ground, resting on its hover field, its nose towards the middle: a pad round Valhalla's edge, level ground elsewhere. Valhalla on Callisto ({valhallaGravity} g) is low and tight: scarps of bare ice with gorges at their feet, ice bridges to fly under, and in the furrow through them the Navy's battlecruiser Steadfast, broken in two. Fly her halls from end to end and the tunnel across each half; most of the round's sponsor probes show up in and around her, and the rest of her is as hard as rock. The Lineae on Europa ({crackGravity} g) have the Crack running through the middle and beside it a field of chaos, tilted rafts of ice to fight among. The ground never hurts: your hover field carries you over the scarps and the rafts and along the canyon's walls; only what stands on it, a hull, a station or a listening post, is as hard as rock. Drop behind a scarp or a raft, into a gorge, into the cruiser or down into the Crack to break a lock: a torpedo has no hover field and goes off on the ground.

Hazards and Alarms

Below the crosshair the flight computer names the worst danger: CAUTION in azure, WARNING in orange, CRITICAL in red with a siren. One line per danger, with an arrow to where it comes from and a countdown where it has one.

Impact

IMPACT means your course hits something sooner than you could flip and brake. Turn or brake now. Below {impactSafe} m/s a rock only scratches the paint; at about {impactKill} m/s it wrecks the ship.

Comets

COMET INBOUND comes {cometWarning} s before a comet reaches the arena, pointing at its head. The head runs down every ship and breaks the smaller rocks in its way; on a rock or a hull heavier than itself it bursts, and that rock breaks and drifts on with the head's momentum, your cover with it. Its two tails, pointing away from the sun, are only dust and gas and harm nobody. The danger is the shower, a band of grit streaking orange along its route behind the head, wide enough to catch a whole fight: each knot of it strikes by its energy, the harder the faster you fly into it, and bursts on whatever solid it meets. Get out of its way, into the lee of a rock or a hull, which stops it, or turn your armored nose into it; never show it your drive. A nova core set off close to the head breaks it into blocks that fly on along its route, as deadly as the head.

Gravity wells

Near the black hole and over Jupiter the PULL gauge shows how much of your drive it would take to get out: CAUTION from {wellCaution} %, WARNING from {wellWarning} %, CRITICAL from {wellCritical} %, and when it would take all of it, NO RETURN. Burn away early: falling, the gauge climbs fast. The speed limit holds only against your own drive; gravity pulls you past {limit} m/s.

The overdrive fires a charge it has stored, the same shove however hurt the drive is, but your drive is cold for {overdriveHeat} s after it. While your drive can still get you out, burn: those seconds cost more than the shove gives, and the cold drive drops you back in. Only when the drive alone no longer gets you out and the shove still does, the alarm says OVERDRIVE · AWAY FROM IT and Nicole calls it: then fire it, nose straight away. That moment comes mostly with a hurt drive, whose point of no return lies farther out, where the pull is weaker. At the Event Horizon, keep your distance, and fight from an orbit around the hole, where it never pulls you in.

Orbits

Near the black hole the flight computer keeps asking where free drift would take you, and shows it under the PULL gauge. STABLE ORBIT: you fall around the hole, never closer than {orbitSafe} m to its center, where its pull alone takes {wellCritical} % of your drive, and never beyond the arena's edge, and the alarms fall silent, for you keep falling and keep missing; a thin blue line draws the whole orbit into space. Pe and Ap give the path's nearest and farthest point over the horizon. With the autopilot on it says ORBIT READY instead: switch it off, and you fly it. A burn, a ram or a hit that takes that margin away brings the alarms back at once.

NO ORBIT · FALLS IN: too slow, the path ends in the hole. NO ORBIT · LEAVES ARENA: too fast, it carries you out. Both give the orbit speed where you are: point your nose across the line to the hole, and burn up to it. A circle a kilometer out takes {orbitKm} m/s, once around in {orbitKmTime} s; at the arena's edge {orbitEdge} m/s. Over Jupiter no orbit fits: its center lies far below the arena, and its air would brake you out of any.

In Jupiter's clouds

The haze hides whatever is in it: just under the deck you see about {seeDeck} km, a kilometer down {seeKm} m, two kilometers down {seeTwoKm} m. Ships turn into shadows, then into nothing. Your laser reaches as far as you see, the radar twice as far, a torpedo's lock two thirds as far. The deck itself is a veil: dive through it, and a chaser above loses sight of you, unless it looks almost straight down.

The air brakes you by your speed squared, {dragDeck} g at the deck at {limit} m/s and ten times as much two kilometers down: a hard turn in the clouds costs speed, a climb out of them lets you have it back. It heats your hull too. At {limit} m/s it gets too hot from {hotDepth} m below the deck on; a kilometer down {safeKm} m/s is safe, two kilometers down {safeTwoKm} m/s. HULL HEAT names the safe speed where you are. The heat locks your laser first, then it burns the hull: slow down, or climb.

Your fins lift you in the air, as a fighter's wings do: turn the nose off your flight, and the air pushes the flight round after it, up to {liftDeck} g at the deck at {limit} m/s, by your speed squared and more the deeper you go, trimmed by the flight computer to {liftMost} g. So in the clouds you turn far tighter the faster you fly: at {limit} m/s on a circle of {carveDeck} m at the deck, where in space it takes {turn} m. The lift costs speed, and your clone feels every g of it. Carve down in the clouds; run above them.

Stay down too long, and the clouds' ammonia and sulfur crust over your radiators: just under the deck they are clogged through in {clogDeck} s, from {clogDepth} m down in {clogDeep} s. Clogged, they shed up to {clogLoss} % less, and the reactor's waste heat, which clean radiators shed without a trace, builds up in them: your laser locks, then the hull burns. RADIATORS CLOGGED shows how far it has got. Over the deck the crust boils off in {unclog} s. The clouds are the place to shake a chaser off, not to stay in; the bold ones follow you down for a while all the same.

In the clouds there is no horizon to fly by, so the flight computer draws one. Under the deck and up to {airTop} m over it, it takes up from the planet's center: a bold line across the view where level lies, the ladder and the bank scale by it, and right of the crosshair your height over the deck with Climbing ▲ or Sinking ▼, sinking under the deck in orange. Lost in the haze? Put the bold line below the crosshair and climb.

In the air, for once, you hear what is outside: the wind roaring past the hull, and thunder after the lightning in the clouds.

The arena's edge

Space has no walls, but the Games do. More than {edge} km from the center the clock runs: {grace} s to get back, or the clone is lost in deep space. Outside, the whole edge glows orange, so the way home is always in sight.

Hull failing

At {hullCritical} hull points or less: HULL FAILING. One more hit and you are dead. Find cover, or a repair probe.

Points, Coins and the Hangar

A kill {kill} (in a race {raceKill}), a close call {closeCall}, a gate {gatePoints}, first place in a race {firstPlace}. Every point is paid out as one Solar Coin at the round's end; training is free and pays nothing.

Kills close together are a show, and the crowd pays for it: a kill within {streak} s of your last one makes a streak. The second earns half a kill more ({streakDouble}), the third and every one after a whole kill more ({streakMore}); the bots' streaks pay alike. A nova's kills already count double and earn no bonus, but they count in the streak; a kill of your own side counts in none, and your clone's death ends it.

In the hangar the coins buy upgrades for both interceptors, three steps each: radiators, thrusters, main drive, torpedo tubes, plating, and the looks (neon trim, drive flame). Besides, they buy gear for each interceptor on its own: the hook ({hookPrice} ☉), the nanobots ({nanobotsPrice} ☉) and the self-destruct ({selfDestructPrice} ☉). Online every upgrade counts for one step at most, so newcomers meet veterans almost as equals; gear and looks count in full, and the bots carry the hook and the nanobots. Complete both interceptors, and the President gives you the Sovereign: a trophy, not a better ship.

Fly the story to its end, and the Magpie is yours for the Games as well: the campaign's ship as Pim finished it, black and chalk white, a neon magpie on each fin. Another trophy, flying like a complete interceptor with all the gear and every look.

Honor among Hosts

The Games run without a central server. The Hosts' browsers build the tournament's network among themselves and agree on what happens by consensus.

The standings (held with Tab, or shown between rounds) name each other Host's ping beside their name: in milliseconds, calm blue until it climbs, orange from {pingWarn} ms and red from {pingCritical} ms. A bot's ping always goes to the lead browser, the one that runs it; yours shows a dash, and so does a bot's while you lead. A small star marks the browser that leads: it runs the round and the bots.

The Host who has been in the room longest is its game master: the HUD says so on their screen, and a star marks them in the standings. From the pause menu the game master can cut a round short for everyone and start a new one, in any arena and mode; everyone's points are paid out on a short scoreboard. The mode chosen holds for the rounds after it, the arenas go on in the room's order. When the game master leaves, the next Host in the room takes over.

The game master is the round's DJ too, the game master (and DJ): the two buttons on their radio box, or the keys , and . (in fullscreen too, where the mouse flies the ship), step the programme back to a song worth hearing again or forward to the next, and the plate Music (DJ) in the pause menu takes the words of a title. The same words always make the same song, so a title heard once plays again, for every Host at the same beat. The next round goes on with the programme from where the DJ left it.
