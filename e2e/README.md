# e2e

Browser tests with Playwright, run against the dev server on port 5173 in Chromium with software WebGL (`playwright.config.ts`). First once: `npm run e2e:install`. Every run starts a dev server of its own checkout and stops with an error when something already answers on the port, so it never tests another checkout's server: `DEV_PORT=5174` moves the server and the tests to another port, `E2E_REUSE=1` tests against a dev server of this checkout that is already running (unset, empty, `0` and `false` mean no).

- `solo.spec.ts` (`npm run e2e`): a solo round of every mode and every course of the flight school start without errors; the hangar sells an upgrade and keeps it; a hangar edited to what no Host can own (from another tab or in the own one) is named on screen until it is put back or the storage is cleared; two ships fly side by side through a gate at 100 m/s, and a dodge into the one at the rim knocks it into the housings, a kill for the kicker (without the dodge both pass).
- `steering.spec.ts` (`npm run e2e`): mouse flight with Playwright's mouse: at 150 m/s with the drive held, the pointer held to the right turns the nose, and with it the flight, round to behind; centered, the ship flies on where its nose points; the input recorder kept the flight.
- `hostrace.spec.ts` (`npm run e2e`): the first gates of a race flown with Playwright's mouse, the pointer pushed the way the crosshair has to turn to reach the racing line ahead as the screen shows it, W held.
- `online.spec.ts` (`npm run e2e`): two or three tabs of one tournament (`?local=1`) see the same world (and the same tints, two Hosts of the same choice told apart), a torpedo of one hits the other, a piece one of them moved alone is where the other has it again once the lead's sync of the field came round, a probe on one's hook comes in to its ship on both and is taken there, they agree on Capture the Flag and the battleship (a laser's hits, those of a torpedo whose clone died after the launch, and a nova core's on every part it reached), and they forgive one impossible nova but cast out a Host whose ship sets them off again and again (the tab named sees its callsign called and stays in its round: it never leaves on the others' word).
- `layout.spec.ts` (`npm run e2e`): the HUD's pieces (round panel, alarms and message, transmission, radar, gauges, radio, kill feed, touch buttons, height and climb, the fixed references, and the names beside the arrows of the rivals off screen, some bunched at one edge), filled as full as they get, never cover each other, and nothing covers the share button, at 1280×720 and 800×360, with keyboard and with touch, in a race and in the flight school.
- `share.spec.ts` (`npm run e2e`): the share button is on the menu and its plates, in a solo round, the flight school and an online room (`?local=1`), on the radio page and in the handbook; its menu opens, copies the game's link (in the online room the room's own), warns for a private room, lets the mouse stop steering while open and pauses nothing.
- `rocks.spec.ts` (`npm run e2e`): a rock drawn as an instance (the belt) shades exactly as the same rock drawn as a mesh of its own (the field), so its baked relief map and the detail map laid over it up close turn with it either way.
- `handbook.spec.ts` (`npm run e2e`): the Pilot's Handbook opens in every language without errors, every number filled in, on a phone's width without scrolling sideways, and a link to a section lands on it.
- `balance.spec.ts` (`npm run balance`, about forty minutes, not part of `npm run e2e`): every mode played out by bots in time-lapse (ten game seconds a second) in every arena, the same arenas every time; a bot of middling skill must hold its own, so a Host can too, judged on its mean over all rounds of a mode: in a deathmatch it scores at least the median and dies at most four times a five-minute round (and the round sees at least six kills), in a race it finishes every round and dies at most three times, in Last Clone Standing its mean place is fifth or better, in Capture the Flag it dies at most five times (and the rounds see a capture), and in the President's Fleet it dies at most three times while the bots sink the battleship in every round. A deathmatch plays each arena 24 times (168 rounds, most of the run's time), Last Clone Standing six times, Capture the Flag twice, the race and the battleship once. The numbers behind it, from 672 deathmatches: the middling bot scores about 130 points over the median on average, but one round's difference scatters by about 550 either way (a bot makes one or two kills a round), so 168 rounds pass the check about 999 times in a thousand, where two rounds would pass it hardly more often than a coin falls heads; the middling bot's mean place in Last Clone Standing lies about 1.1 inside the limit, with a scatter of about 1.8 places a round, so 42 rounds keep it inside. Seven rounds run side by side, each on a page of its own (`BALANCE_PAGES=<n>` for fewer on a busy or small machine). Each round's development log (`src/devlog.ts`) goes with the result: its JSON in the test's folder under `test-results/` (`<mode>-<round>-<arena>.json`, attached to the report too), what the checks judged with every round's standings, length and deaths by cause in `<mode>.json` beside them, its summary in the annotations, and the deaths by cause in the detail of a failed check. Read a log with `node -e` or any JSON viewer: `summary.deaths`, `summary.afterDecision`, and the `death` events with the decisions before them say what killed the bots. On a busy machine the time-lapse plays slower: `BALANCE_SLOW=4 npm run balance` gives each round four times as long. `BALANCE_ARENA=hole npm run balance` plays only that arena's rounds (its id in `src/world/arenas.ts`), to look at one arena after a change to it; the checks then judge its rounds alone, fewer of them, so a mean scatters more than over every arena; `-g race` (a word of the test's title) plays one mode.
- `soak.spec.ts` (`npm run soak`, about a quarter of an hour, not part of `npm run e2e`): three honest tabs play a round of every mode in turn (each brought to its end after `SOAK_ROUND` seconds, 100 by default) with the lead's bots, every Host's clone flown by a wild hand on the keys (stick, drive, flips, dodges, hook, overdrive, laser, torpedoes), the tabs' audio suspended (three GPU-less tabs play too slowly with it) and up to four minutes for each tab to load on a busy machine; no tab may report another or name a cheater. Every report a tab sends is recorded for the whole test as it goes out (its session's `sendCheat` hooked), because the tournament's own list of the round's reports (`game.online.reported`) starts over with each round, before the round's verdict is read. Every failed check (`game.online.suspicions`, also in the development log as `suspicion`) and how close each check came to its bound (`game.online.margins`, 1: on it) go into `soak.json` in the test's folder and onto the console.
- `bench.spec.ts` (`npm run bench`, about ten minutes, not part of `npm run e2e`): the benchmark (`src/bench.ts`, README "Performance") in the test browser; `BENCH_SECONDS=<s>` per measurement (default 3), `PROFILE=pixel8` as the reference phone, `BENCH_ONLY=quality` (or other sections, comma-separated) for only some sections. The table goes to the console, the JSON and the table to the test's folder under `test-results/` (`bench.json`, `bench.txt`). It runs against the dev server (`webServer` in `playwright.config.ts`; with `E2E_REUSE=1` one already up on the port): a file saved in the worktree while a run is in flight reaches the page through Vite's hot reload mid-measurement and spoils it, so a run is left to finish (or stopped) before the next edit.
- `profiles.ts`: the devices the benchmark can pretend to be (`PROFILE=<name>`). `pixel8`: the Pixel 8 held sideways, 915 × 412 CSS pixels at a ratio of 2.625 (2400 × 1080 device pixels), touch, and its CPU speed in a browser.
- `game.ts`: shared helpers (open the game with the low quality tier pinned, launch a solo round, play a few seconds) and the game as it hangs on the window (`window.game`).

**The test browser.** Chromium draws WebGL in software (swiftshader) and composites the page without the GPU (`--disable-gpu-compositing` in `playwright.config.ts`): compositing the HUD's layers and the neon's glows in software GL held every tab to a few frames a second and slowed a second tab's start so much that the online tests timed out. The helpers also switch the scene's and the title screen's drawing off; the tests watch the game's state, not its picture.

**The Pixel 8 profile and its limits.** The screen and touch are the phone's. The CPU is slowed with `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate` to the phone's speed in Speedometer 2.0: the Pixel 8 makes 136 runs/min in Chrome 116 (Notebookcheck's review of the Pixel 8). The profile throttles by 6.3, calibrated with Speedometer 2.0 (browserbench.org) in the test browser on the development machine (an Apple M4, otherwise quiet), two runs per rate, in runs/min:

| Throttling | 1 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 6.5 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Run 1 | 855 | 229 | 172 | 154 | 132 | 124 | 110 |
| Run 2 | 862 | 228 | 171 | 147 | 126 | 129 | 103 |

136 runs/min lies between 6 (150 on average) and 6.5 (129): about 6.3. On another machine, calibrate anew the same way. The benchmark's tables measured with the profile are in the README's section "Performance". The GPU cannot be slowed: swiftshader is a slow GPU of its own kind, so the frame times under the profile only compare features and rungs with each other at the phone's pixel count, where fill rate decides; the phone's own numbers come from `?bench` on the phone.

Run the browser tests before pushing changes to the network or the round's flow, and the balance runs after changes to bots, weapons, damage or a mode's rules. A red balance run is read from its logs before anything is changed: with every arena and enough rounds per mode, it is rarely chance.
